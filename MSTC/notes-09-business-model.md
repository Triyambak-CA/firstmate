## 2.1 E-Commerce segment - what it is

One line: MSTC operates online electronic-auction and e-procurement platforms on which government departments, PSUs, banks and, less commonly, private sellers list scrap, surplus assets, mineral/coal rights, seized goods, land, or NPA-backed collateral for competitive online bidding, earning a service/commission charge on the value transacted rather than owning or trading the underlying goods itself (except for the residual, now-exited, Trading segment).

## 2.3 Value-chain position summary (qualitative, cross-segment)

- Inputs/suppliers: MSTC does not source physical inputs for its core e-commerce business; the "inputs" are (a) the assets/rights government/PSU sellers list (scrap, mineral blocks, seized goods, land, NPA collateral) and (b) the bidder pool that shows up to bid, which MSTC must keep registered, KYC'd, and liquid enough to clear each auction at a fair price. For the now-exited Trading segment, MSTC was a principal buyer/seller of physical scrap and metals - that inventory-risk model is gone as of Q1 FY27.
- What MSTC actually owns/does (the value-add): platform operation (bidder registration, EMD collection/verification, bid management, price discovery, settlement coordination), not asset ownership, valuation, or logistics of the underlying goods (sellers/buyers handle physical delivery). MSTC's moat, such as it is, is the CVC/CAG-linked nomination as a preferred/mandated e-auction agency for PSU asset disposal (LOW-CONFIDENCE, needs primary policy-document confirmation) plus incumbency/switching-cost inertia with large recurring government sellers - both are administrative/regulatory moats, not technology or network-effect moats, which is exactly why they are exposed to the "government moves the mandate elsewhere" risk documented in notes-10.
- Customers/channel: government ministries, PSUs, public-sector banks (via IBA/PSB Alliance channel, now weakening - see 2.1.6), and state governments, reached through MSTC's own e-commerce portal (mstcecommerce.com) and sector-specific sub-portals (coal, mineral block, IBAPI/bank-property).
- End use: for scrap/mineral/coal auctions, the buyer-side end use is industrial input (steel mills, traders, recyclers) sourcing raw material at auction-discovered prices; for land/bank-NPA auctions, end use is asset acquisition by property/investment buyers.

## 2.4 UNKNOWN UNKNOWNS - MSTC-specific

1. Government-nomination dependence / GeM and ministry-portal disintermediation risk: MSTC's core moat is administrative nomination (PSU scrap-disposal rules, ministry auction mandates), not a durable technology or network-effect advantage. The Railways precedent (see notes-10: Railways built its own IREPS in-house rather than pay MSTC's proposed 1.5-2% charge, a decision made years ago and never reversed) and the eBKray-to-PSB-Alliance/BAANKNET migration (see 2.1.6 and notes-10) are not hypothetical risks - they are CONFIRMED PRIOR INSTANCES of exactly this disintermediation happening to MSTC. The open question is not whether this can happen but how much of the remaining book (Coal India, Ministry of Mines/Coal, state land mandates, Customs/DRI, MoD) is similarly exposed to GeM absorption or a ministry building its own portal once volumes justify the switching cost - NOT FOUND, no forward-looking disclosure on this risk found in company material.
2. Take-rate compression on tender renewal: directly evidenced by the Mar-2025 Coal India work order being explicitly capped at "40% of estimated work value," i.e. a mandate that was presumably closer to fully MSTC's in the past is now openly shared/competitively tendered. Whether this pattern is spreading to other large mandates (Ministry of Mines, state land auctions) is NOT FOUND from available sources.
3. Commodity-price sensitivity of GMV: scrap/coal/mineral prices directly set the rupee value of what's being auctioned, so GMV (and therefore revenue, given the percentage-fee model) rises and falls with commodity cycles that MSTC does not control and cannot hedge - this is a structural, undiversifiable input to revenue that behaves like a commodity-linked royalty rather than an operating business with cost control. No company disclosure of commodity-price sensitivity/beta was found (NOT FOUND) - this is an analytical inference from the business model, not a disclosed risk factor.
4. Revenue is a derivative of OTHER people's volumes: MSTC's growth is capped by how much scrap/coal/minerals/NPA property government and PSU counterparties choose to auction in a given year - a decision driven by those counterparties' own production cycles, divestment/monetisation appetite, and budget-cycle timing, none of which MSTC controls or can forecast independently. This makes MSTC's own "growth strategy" (see notes-10) largely a story about capturing a larger share of a demand pool set by others, not about creating new demand.
5. Technology platform obsolescence: MSTC's core asset is its e-auction software/portal; no disclosure found (NOT FOUND) on the age of the underlying platform, ongoing technology capex, cybersecurity posture, or uptime/reliability track record - all of which matter for retaining nominated-mandate status against newer government portals (GeM, ministry-built alternatives) built on more modern stacks. This is exactly the kind of gap a scuttlebutt pass (bidder complaints about portal quality/downtime) should probe - see notes-11, where this could not be run down this session due to exhausted search budget.
6. Legacy Trading receivables tail: even with the Trading segment operationally exited as of Q1 FY27, any residual receivables, disputed claims, or contingent liabilities from the wound-down Trading book are a balance-sheet tail risk not quantified here - NOT FOUND (needs the FY26 annual report's contingent-liabilities and trade-receivables-ageing notes).
7. Disinvestment overhang: MSTC is a Government of India PSU under the Ministry of Steel; as with any listed CPSE, government stake-sale/disinvestment intent (via DIPAM) is a standing overhang on the stock and can also affect strategic direction (e.g. pressure to monetise land/assets, as seen in the Telangana land-auction mandate MSTC itself executed for TSIIC in Q1 FY27 - MSTC is on both sides of this theme, as an operator of land-monetisation auctions for others and as a CPSE potentially subject to the same policy pressure). dipam.gov.in could not be accessed this session to check MSTC's current listed disinvestment/stake-sale status - NOT FOUND, flag for direct DIPAM website/press-release check.
