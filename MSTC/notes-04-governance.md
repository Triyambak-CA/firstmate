## 5. Board composition and SEBI LODR independent-director compliance - KEY GOVERNANCE FINDING

MSTC has a **documented, multi-quarter history of non-compliance with SEBI LODR Regulation 17(1)**
(minimum independent directors, including the mandatory woman independent director), for which
both BSE and NSE have levied fines. Because MSTC is a Government company, the President of India
(via the Ministry of Steel) - not the company's own board or management - holds the sole authority
to appoint (including independent) directors. Every fine disclosure found has MSTC's board framing
the non-compliance as "beyond management's control" and formally requesting a waiver.

**Fines identified (each is a separate BSE/NSE disclosure; do not assume these are the complete
set - the search budget ran out before a full quarter-by-quarter reconstruction back to FY22 could
be completed):**

| Quarter (violation) | Exchange | Fine amount | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|
| Quarter ended 30-Jun-2025 (Q1 FY26) | BSE | Rs 8,90,900 (incl. GST) | Explicitly tied to Reg 17(1) non-compliance. |
| Q3 FY26 | BSE | Rs 5,42,800 | "Board composition non-compliance." |
| Q4 FY26 | BSE | Rs 5.31 lakh | "Board non-compliance." |
| Unspecified quarter | NSE | Rs 12 lakh | Headline snippet read "STC fined ₹12 lakh by NSE for independent director gap" - almost certainly a truncated "MSTC" but **not 100% certain from the snippet alone; exact quarter NOT FOUND.** |

Despite the remediation below (Apr-Jun 2025), fines for **Q3 FY26 and Q4 FY26** (i.e. periods
*after* the remediation) were still being disclosed - this needs reconciling: either the fines
relate to the earlier non-compliant quarters and were simply disclosed/settled later, or MSTC
fell out of compliance again after the June-2025 fix (e.g., due to the Director (Commercial)
vacancy from 01-Nov-2026, or another director's term lapsing). **NOT FOUND: a clean explanation
of why fines continued into Q3/Q4 FY26 after the stated June-2025 fix - flag for direct reading of
each BSE filing's stated violation period.**

**Remediation (Apr-Jun 2025):** the Ministry of Steel appointed three new Non-Official Independent
Directors:
- Shri Ramesh Kumar Soni (DIN 09399355), w.e.f. 15-Apr-2025.
- Smt. Alka Chandrakar (DIN 11111923).
- Shri Chandra Shekhar Baghel (DIN 11111187).

This brought MSTC into compliance with Reg 17(1), including the woman-independent-director
requirement, and all statutory board committees (Reg 18(1) Audit, Reg 19 Nomination &
Remuneration, Reg 20 Stakeholders Relationship, Reg 21(2) Risk Management) were reconstituted
accordingly.

**Exact current board size and independent-director count/ratio: NOT FOUND** as a clean, dated
number in this session. Given MSTC has an executive Chairman (the CMD chairs the board and is
whole-time), Reg 17(1) requires independent directors to be at least half the board (not the
lower one-third threshold that applies to a non-executive-chairman board) - this makes the
multi-year vacancy problem more consequential than it would be for a company with a non-executive
chair. Re-confirm the current total board strength and ID count directly from the FY26 annual

### The legacy bad-debt / "trading business" write-off - IMPORTANT RECONCILIATION WITH BRIEF

The brief's framing describes "a trading/financing business for steel/coal buyers that blew up
with massive bad debts around FY18-FY20." **What this research actually found is a different,
though similarly-scaled, historical event, and the two should not be conflated:**

1. **The core bad-debt disaster traces to a 2008-09 gold-jewellery export scheme**, not a
   steel/coal financing book. Under a duty-exemption/nominated-agency gold-import scheme (the
   same general mechanism MMTC/STC/PEC operated under, and which a 2015 CAG review separately
   criticized those sister PSUs for), MSTC's arrangement involved **six associate exporters**
   shipping gold jewellery worth **Rs 638.20 crore** to **47 foreign buyers**, of whom **46
   defaulted on payment**. A CBI official in **October 2010** described the resulting scam as
   worth "Rs 1,400 crore-plus," with jewellery traders as the prime suspects for defrauding MSTC.
   MSTC subsequently filed legal suits aggregating **Rs 658.40 crore** against the 46 defaulting
   buyers across various forums (case status/recoveries: NOT FOUND).
2. **The P&L reckoning for this old exposure was finally taken just before/around the March-2019
   IPO.** Per DRHP-era management commentary found: MSTC "opted for aggressive write-offs of its
   FY11 and FY12 transactions" as balance-sheet clean-up ahead of listing, and **FY19 provisioning
   against long-due receivables was Rs 542.08 crore** (materially lower write-offs/provisions
   followed in FY20). This matches the loss-making results found for the surrounding period: FY18
   loss of Rs (6.48) crore, and a H1 FY19 loss of Rs (15.88) crore on turnover of Rs 1,491.55
   crore.
3. **Accountability: NOT FOUND.** The CBI investigation identified above targeted the external
   jewellery traders/exporters, not MSTC's own management, board, or the officials who approved
   or oversaw the scheme. No source found in this session shows any MSTC director, CMD, or senior
   officer being investigated, disciplined, or held accountable for the scheme's approval or for
   the decade-long delay in recognizing the loss. **This is itself a governance finding worth
   stating plainly: a Rs 540-660 crore write-off/litigation event with no identified internal
   accountability trail in the public record reviewed.**
4. **A distinct, separate dynamic - not itself a "blow-up" - is the secular decline of MSTC's core
   steel/ferro-alloy/scrap trading-intermediation business over 2017-2020**, as government
   procurement volumes migrated to other centralized portals and eroded MSTC's traditional
   thin-margin trading role. This is a structural revenue-mix shift, not a bad-debt event.
5. **This research did NOT find a distinctly separate steel/coal-buyer bad-debt blow-up of
   comparable scale specifically in the FY18-FY20 window**, beyond the gold-export write-off
   whose provisioning happened to land in FY19. If the brief's framing is based on a specific
   other source (e.g., a particular annual report note on steel/coal customer defaults), that
   source was not surfaced here - **flag this as a reconciliation gap requiring a targeted
   re-search of MSTC FY18-FY20 annual report notes on trade receivables/provisions specifically
   for steel and coal-sector customers**, since it is possible both a gold-export writeoff AND a
   separate steel/coal bad-debt episode occurred and this research only surfaced the former.
