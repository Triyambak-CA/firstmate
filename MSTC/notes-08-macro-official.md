## 20.1 THE SINGLE MOST IMPORTANT FACT: GeM's GMV vs MSTC's GMV

Repeated here for prominence (full detail and sourcing in notes-06-industry.md section 1.1):

- **GeM FY26 GMV: Rs 5.03 lakh crore** (Rs 5,03,000 crore), DOWN 7.4% YoY - the first observed annual decline in the GeM GMV series (FY22 through FY26) in this research.
- **MSTC FY26 "value of goods transacted": Rs 79,737 crore** (Rs 0.80 lakh crore), which itself represents a sharp deceleration/decline from FY25's Rs 89,823 crore, and from a 9M FY26 run-rate implying a weak H1 (H1 FY26 alone reportedly fell 36.65% YoY to Rs 30,167 crore on softening scrap prices).
- **GeM is roughly 6.3x the size of MSTC's own platform GMV** in FY26. GeM's cumulative GMV since its 2016 inception has reached **Rs 18.4 lakh crore** (PIB, via IBEF citation, "US$197.72 billion").
- Both platforms' GMV **declined or decelerated sharply in FY26** - this is a genuinely important, probably underappreciated macro signal: it is not simply "GeM grows while MSTC struggles" - the broader government e-platform GMV pool (at least on the two largest platforms measured here) contracted in FY26. Plausible shared drivers: lower CPSE capital/procurement spending (explicitly cited as the GeM FY26 decline driver) and softening commodity/scrap prices (explicitly cited as the MSTC H1 FY26 decline driver) - these are two different mechanisms (procurement spend vs. commodity price) affecting two different platforms in the same direction in the same year, which could be coincidental or could reflect a shared macro slowdown in government-linked capex/disposal activity. NOT FOUND: any single official source connecting the two declines causally - flag as an analyst hypothesis, not a confirmed finding.

- **Combined FY27 disinvestment + asset-monetisation target: Rs 80,000 crore** (multiple financial-media sources reporting on the FY27 Budget), a sharp step-up from FY26, where the government **originally budgeted Rs 47,000 crore but achieved only around Rs 34,000 crore on a revised-estimate basis** ("muted execution" cited as the reason) - **flag the FY27 target explicitly as a budget target/aspiration, not a committed or historically-typical outcome**, given FY26's shortfall against its own original target.
- FY27 disinvestment activity as of the search date includes stake reductions in **six CPSEs: Central Bank of India, Coal India, NHPC, NLC India, General Insurance Corporation (GIC), and Indian Railway Finance Corporation (IRFC)**, with roughly **Rs 20,000 crore raised so far** in FY27 as of the source's reporting date (undated precisely within FY27 in the retrieved snippet - treat as a mid-year, not final, figure). Separately, the **Expression-of-Interest stage has been completed for strategic sale of six other CPSEs: IDBI Bank, NMDC Steel, HLL Lifecare, Projects & Development India Ltd (PDIL), BEML, and Shipping Corporation of India (SCI).** Relevance to MSTC: disinvestment/strategic-sale transactions are generally executed via investment-bank-run processes, not through MSTC's e-auction platform, so this pipeline is **not a direct MSTC revenue driver** in most cases - but MSTC's own asset-disposal mandate (surplus land, scrap, obsolete plant/machinery at PSUs undergoing sale or wind-down) could see incremental volume from PSUs preparing for strategic sale. NOT FOUND: any specific linkage stated between this disinvestment pipeline and MSTC's e-auction volumes.
- **NMP 2.0 (National Monetisation Pipeline 2.0), launched by FM Sitharaman** (PIB PRID=2231900) - covers FY 2025-26 to FY 2029-30, with an aggregate potential of **Rs 16.72 lakh crore** (including private-sector investment of Rs 5.8 lakh crore, "2.6x higher than NMP 1.0" per one summary). Year-wise award-target phasing: **FY26 Rs 2.49 lakh crore, FY27 Rs 3.26 lakh crore, FY28 Rs 3.46 lakh crore, FY29 Rs 3.68 lakh crore, FY30 Rs 3.81 lakh crore.** Sectoral breakdown most relevant to MSTC's world: **coal Rs 2.16 lakh crore, mines Rs 1 lakh crore** (also highways/logistics Rs 4.42 lakh crore, railways Rs 2.62 lakh crore, power Rs 2.76 lakh crore, ports Rs 2.63 lakh crore, and smaller allocations elsewhere). **Important caveat/ministerial-projection flag: NMP asset-monetisation is primarily structured around long-tenure concessions/TOT (toll-operate-transfer) and leasing models for infrastructure assets, not one-time physical-asset disposal auctions** - MSTC's core e-auction competency (scrap, minerals, seized goods, one-time asset sales) is not the same mechanism as NMP's concession-based monetisation, so **direct MSTC participation in NMP 2.0 execution is NOT CONFIRMED and should not be assumed** despite the topical adjacency (both are "government asset value realisation" themes). Label all NMP 2.0 figures explicitly as **five-year targets set by government, not achieved or even reliably forecast outcomes** - NMP 1.0's own track record included well-documented shortfalls against target in several sectors (e.g., Railways' asset-monetisation underperformance is referenced in passing in one search snippet, "Indian railways unlikely to meet Rs 1.52 lakh crore asset monetisation target" - a prior-pipeline data point, not NMP 2.0-specific, but relevant base-rate context for skepticism on NMP 2.0 targets).
- **Prior year's Budget (2025-26) relevant scrap/circular-economy measure**: customs duties on **critical-mineral waste and scrap were eliminated**, reducing input costs for Indian recyclers (cited via Down To Earth/EESI-style secondary coverage of the Survey's retrospective). A **Rs 1,500 crore scheme, rolled out October 2025**, targets recovery of critical minerals (lithium, cobalt, nickel, etc.) from secondary resources - end-of-life batteries, e-waste, industrial scrap - under the **National Critical Mineral Mission**, which also plans to promote mineral recovery from red mud/fly ash and an incentive scheme for mineral-recycling centres. Relevance to MSTC: this is upstream/adjacent to MSTC's e-waste and scrap-auction verticals (more feedstock for recycling could mean more auctionable scrap volume over time), but **no direct MSTC linkage or budget allocation was found** - flag as thematically relevant, not confirmed as a direct revenue driver.

## 20.5 Volume drivers - India steel scrap, coal production, mineral block auctions (per-year data)

(Full detail and sourcing already captured in notes-06-industry.md sections 1.2 and 1.4 - summarised here for the macro-data record.)

**Steel scrap:**
- FY26 India ferrous scrap consumption: **~41 million tonnes**, +17% YoY from ~35 Mt in FY25 (SteelOrbis, citing trade data).
- FY26 domestic scrap generation: **32 million tonnes**, +22% YoY.
- FY26 scrap-based crude steel production: **38 million tonnes**, +16% YoY (vs +11% YoY for overall crude steel production - scrap-based route growing faster than the whole industry).
- Scrap imports: typically 6-8 million tonnes/year, fluctuating with Basic Customs Duty policy and global price differentials; **FY26 imports fell ~20% YoY** on cheaper domestic alternatives.

**Coal production:**
- All-India coal production, FY25 (actual): **1,047.6 million tonnes (all-time high)**.
- All-India coal production, FY26 (Ministry of Coal target): **1,150.39 million tonnes (~+10% YoY target)**.
- Coal India Limited (CIL) alone, FY25 (actual): **781.1 million tonnes**, ~7% below CIL's own FY25 target (i.e., CIL itself under-delivered against its target even as all-India production hit a record, implying non-CIL/captive producers over-delivered).
- CIL FY26 target: reported as **868 million tonnes by one source and 875 million tonnes (~76.06% of the all-India target) by another** - **flag as an unreconciled ~7 Mt discrepancy between sources**, likely a revised-vs-original-target distinction; use the range, not a single point figure, pending primary Coal Ministry confirmation.

**Mineral block auctions (MMDR Act 1957 regime):**
- FY22-FY25 cumulative: **364 blocks auctioned**, averaging **~90 blocks/year**.
- FY26: **record 212 blocks auctioned** (an in-year checkpoint around 19-Mar-2026 cited 200 blocks: 123 Mining Lease + 77 Composite Licence; the 212 figure is the reported final full-FY26 count, including 22 critical/strategic mineral blocks) - **more than double the FY22-FY25 annual average**, a clear step-change acceleration.
- Operationalisation (mines actually producing, the real economic-activation lag behind an auction): FY26 alone added **36 operationalised blocks (28 greenfield + 8 brownfield)**, versus a **cumulative 58 blocks (20 greenfield + 38 brownfield) operationalised across the entire FY16-FY25 decade-long period** - i.e., the pace of blocks becoming productive is now (FY26) running at roughly 60% of the *entire prior decade's cumulative total* in a single year. This is the clearest, cleanest structural tailwind identified in this research for MSTC's mineral-auction-linked fee income, assuming MSTC retains its nominated-provider role in the relevant states (see notes-06 section 1.2 and 1.7 for the contestability caveat).

## 20.6 Contradictions between official data and MSTC management commentary

1. **MSTC management attributes the H1 FY26 GMV decline (-36.65% YoY) to "softening scrap prices"** - yet official steel-sector data (SteelOrbis/IBEF-adjacent) shows **domestic scrap generation volumes rising strongly in FY26 (+22% YoY) and scrap-based steel production rising (+16% YoY)**. This is not necessarily a contradiction (volume can rise while price falls, and MSTC's ad-valorem fee model is geared to *value* transacted, not physical tonnage), but it does mean **management's "scrap prices" framing is a partial explanation** - it implicitly concedes that physical activity in the scrap market was not the problem (volumes grew), only prices were soft. This distinction matters for forecasting: if scrap volumes keep growing (as official data suggests they will, given rising domestic generation), a price recovery alone could restore MSTC's GMV growth without needing any volume tailwind - a lower bar than management's framing might suggest at first read.
2. **GeM's FY26 GMV decline is officially attributed to "lower order value from CPSEs"** - this is a demand-side (buyer) explanation specific to GeM's PSU/CPSE procurement customers, and by its nature says nothing directly about MSTC (a different platform, different customer base, different transaction type - disposal/auction vs. procurement/purchase). Treat any temptation to read GeM's CPSE-driven decline as directly explanatory of MSTC's own decline as **unsupported** - the two platforms' declines, while contemporaneous, have officially-stated causes that are mechanistically distinct (CPSE capex/procurement softness for GeM; scrap price softness for MSTC). This is worth stating plainly because a lazy read of "both platforms declined in FY26" could wrongly imply a single shared cause when the two official explanations point to different mechanisms.
3. **MSTC's FY26 revenue grew (+18.88% YoY, Rs 369.66 crore from operations) while net profit fell sharply (-46.34% YoY, Rs 218.43 crore from Rs 407.07 crore)** - this is an internal MSTC contradiction (revenue up, profit down) rather than an official-data-vs-management contradiction per se, but it is the single most important number to reconcile in any deeper dive: it implies either (a) FY25's Rs 407.07 crore profit included a large one-off/exceptional gain not repeated in FY26 (common for PSUs with investment-income volatility, given MSTC's net-cash balance sheet), (b) FY26 saw a margin-compressing cost increase despite revenue growth, or (c) some combination. **NOT FOUND: a specific management explanation for this divergence in the secondary sources retrieved this session** - this is a priority item for the next research pass, ideally from the FY26 results earnings-call transcript or investor presentation directly.
