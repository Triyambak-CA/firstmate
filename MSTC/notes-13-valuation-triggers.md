AUDIT - why several of these would MISLEAD a purely mechanical screen on MSTC specifically:

1. ROE/ROCE look attractively high (mid-to-high-20s % on the rough ROE estimate above) partly BECAUSE the equity base is thin relative to market cap (P/B ~6x per 12.2) - a small denominator inflates the ratio. A meaningful share of reported profit is treasury/other income earned on the large cash-and-investments pile at low-risk deposit-type yields, not operating return on the e-auction platform's productive capital. A screen that reads "high ROE = high-quality compounder" without decomposing operating vs. treasury income will overstate the business's true capital efficiency.

2. Debt/Equity near-zero (~17.5%, effectively no meaningful leverage) combined with 0% promoter pledge would screen as "maximally safe, debt-free PSU." This is TRUE on financial debt, but a standard D/E ratio completely ignores the large EMD/customer-deposit float sitting in current liabilities (see 12.2's ~Rs 1,030cr inferred "other liabilities"). This is not financial risk in the leverage sense, but it DOES mean a large share of what shows up as balance-sheet "cash and investments" is not shareholder-free capital - exactly the trap a naive "debt-free and cash-rich" screen falls into for MSTC. This is the single most important audit point and is discussed at length in 12.2.

3. The FSNL divestment (MSTC's sale of its stake in subsidiary Ferro Scrap Nigam Ltd, government approval reported ~20-Sep-2024 for ~Rs 320cr, completion around Sep-2024) is a ONE-OFF, non-recurring item that sits inside MSTC's FY24/FY25 consolidated financials. Any 5-year sales/profit CAGR screen that spans this window (which is exactly the window a "5-yr CAGR" field on a stock screener would cover as of Sep-2026) will partially capture this divestment-related gain/restructuring rather than pure recurring e-auction/e-commerce operating growth - inflating the apparent historical growth rate. The subsequent FY26 profit decline of 45% YoY (see 12.2/12.6) is itself consistent with FY25's base having been flattered by non-recurring items that are now normalizing away; a mechanical CAGR field would not surface this explanation on its own.

4. Promoter holding 64.74-64.75% and 0% pledge read as "textbook safe, no promoter-selling risk" on a screener. True as far as it goes, but it says nothing about the COMPOSITION of the ~35% non-promoter float, which per notes-12 section 13 is overwhelmingly retail (~29-30% of the whole company) with only ~5-6% combined institutional ownership and mutual funds near-nil (~0.10%). A large, fast-arriving retail base (consistent with the stock's 69.7% six-month and 31.64% one-year price moves) is a crowding/froth risk that a "promoter holding stable, no pledge" screen field does not capture at all - if anything it can create false comfort.

5. A generic "market cap vs. net cash" screen (the classic "cash exceeds X% of market cap, looks cheap" style filter) is the metric MOST LIKELY TO MISLEAD on MSTC specifically, for the reason set out at length in 12.2: the EXACT EMD/customer-deposit liability figure is NOT FOUND here, but is plausibly a large share of the ~Rs 1,030cr of non-debt "other liabilities" inferred from the balance sheet snapshot, given MSTC's e-auction business model requires holding bidder EMDs. Any screen or naive analysis that nets the FULL disclosed cash-and-investments balance against market cap without adjusting for this liability will overstate how much "true" ex-cash discount MSTC is trading at. THIS IS THE SINGLE MOST IMPORTANT VALUATION CAVEAT IN THIS FILE and should be the first thing checked/corrected once the primary annual report's balance-sheet notes are accessible.
