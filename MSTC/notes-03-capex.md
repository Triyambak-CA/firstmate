## 3. Investments in JV/associates (Mahindra JV)

- Mahindra MSTC Recycling Private Limited ("MMRPL," operating brand CERO) - confirmed as a 50:50 joint
  venture with Mahindra Accelo Limited (Mahindra Group), incorporated 16-Dec-2016, operating India's
  first authorized/organized end-of-life-vehicle (ELV) recycling facility at Greater Noida, with a
  "zero pollution" design philosophy (the name Cero = "zero" in Spanish, per the JV's own positioning).
  ISO 9001, ISO 14001 and OHSAS 18001 certified per the JV's own materials.
- Expansion: CERO/MMRPL has stated plans (per Autocar Professional interview coverage of CERO's Sumit
  Issar) to expand capacity toward dismantling up to a million end-of-life vehicles by 2026-27, with
  a Government of Maharashtra MoU for additional scrapping centres in that state, and a partnership with

## 4. Use of FSNL sale proceeds (~Rs 320 crore) - dividend, buyback, or hoarded?

- FSNL (100% subsidiary) was divested to Konoike Transport Co. Ltd (Japan) via a Share Purchase Agreement
  signed 24-Oct-2024, at a consideration of Rs 320 crore, against an independently-assessed Reserve Price
  of Rs 262 crore (both figures corroborated by PIB press-release coverage and independent business-media
  reporting surfaced via search - this is one of the higher-confidence figures in this whole research
  pass). The disinvestment process itself traces back to a 2016 CCEA in-principle approval, with the
  RFP/SPA process formally run from January 2024 (two sealed bids received by 31-Jan-2024).
- A one-time exceptional gain from the sale was booked in the Dec-2024 quarter (Q3 FY25), reported with
  three DIFFERENT rupee values across different sources found (Rs 273.54 crore / Rs 301.69 crore /
  Rs 263.19 crore - see notes-01 for the full discussion of this inconsistency). None of the three could
  be confirmed as definitively correct in this session; they may reflect gain-before-tax vs after-tax vs
  a restated/updated figure, or simple data-source error. NEEDS VERIFICATION against the actual Q3 FY25
  results filing.
- Distribution evidence: a special/large dividend of Rs 32.00 per share is referenced in search results,
  associated (with some date ambiguity - see notes-01 Section 8) with the FY24-25 dividend cycle, and the
  search synthesis itself speculatively links it to the FSNL proceeds. This is DIRECTIONALLY PLAUSIBLE -
  a PSU receiving a ~Rs 320 crore one-off cash inflow from a government-directed strategic disinvestment,
  and then paying an unusually large special dividend in the following months, is a very common pattern
  for Indian CPSEs (which face DIPAM/Ministry of Finance dividend-policy pressure to distribute
  windfall/non-core-asset-sale proceeds rather than retain them) - but the LINK ITSELF was not confirmed
  from a primary source (no BSE corporate-action filing or annual report cash-flow "financing activities"
  note was accessible to confirm either the special dividend's exact quantum/date or its stated purpose).
- Reinvestment evidence: NO evidence was found of the FSNL proceeds being redeployed into capex, the
  Mahindra JV, or any new operating initiative. The one new-initiative data point found - MSTC "plans
  travel business entry" (mentioned alongside the FY26 results and Rs 8.10 final dividend announcement,
  per Whalesbook coverage headlines) - is intriguingly named but has NO financial detail attached
  (investment size, structure, timeline) in anything found in this session. This is worth flagging as a
  new, as-yet-unquantified diversification move that a forensic reviewer should watch for capital
  allocation discipline (a PSU e-auction company entering "travel business" is a notable scope expansion
  that deserves scrutiny on strategic rationale and capital committed, once details are available).
- CASH BALANCE trend (a proxy for "hoarded" vs "distributed"): notes-01 shows total assets declining
  modestly from ~Rs 2,249 cr (around FY24/25) to ~Rs 2,118 cr (Dec-2025), and one possible reading of the
  conflicting net-worth figures (Rs ~900 cr FY24 vs Rs ~738 cr Dec-2025) is that FY25-FY26 dividend
  outflows (including the special dividend) EXCEEDED retained profit over that period - i.e. the evidence
  leans toward "distributed" rather than "hoarded," but this rests on balance-sheet figures already
  flagged as internally inconsdistent in notes-01 and should not be treated as conclusive.
- CONCLUSION: the weight of (admittedly incomplete and partly unconfirmed) evidence points toward the
  FSNL proceeds having been substantially DISTRIBUTED to shareholders via an outsized dividend rather than
  reinvested in capex or hoarded on the balance sheet, consistent with typical CPSE capital-allocation
  behavior under government dividend-policy pressure - but this conclusion should be held with MODERATE,
  not high, confidence given the sourcing gaps described above, and should be confirmed against the
  FY25 Cash Flow Statement's "Dividends Paid" line and the FY25 annual report's discussion of use of
  disinvestment proceeds (if any such discussion exists - PSUs sometimes address this explicitly in the
  Directors' Report or MD&A).

  competitive asset, exactly the risk the task brief asked to be investigated). The data gathered in this
  session cannot distinguish between these two readings with confidence.

## 6. Technology / IT platform investment (qualitative only - no capitalised figures found)

- MSTC's e-auction portal has been running since 2002 and the company describes itself (via its own
  website content, surfaced through search) as having conducted "more than 200,000 events of different
  types," giving it the experience base to build "customised auction platforms to suit client
  requirements."
- The platform is described as integrated with multiple banks for e-payment, and as having a "Global EMD
  Management System" for automated EMD handling during bidding, and as being "PKI enabled."
- MSTC states it develops and maintains in-house software including HRMS, Accounting, Inventory
  Management, Project Management, and an "Online Draw" system, alongside the core e-auction/e-procurement
  platforms.
- A newer e-Procurement platform ("Version 3") was stated to have launched in 2020, described as
  "enriched with various advanced user-friendly features."
- NONE of this qualitative description comes with a capitalised software/intangible-assets rupee figure,
  an annual IT spend figure, or a headcount/vendor-spend figure for the technology function. This
  opacity is itself a relevant forensic observation: a company whose core competitive asset is a
  software platform, but which discloses essentially nothing publicly about the scale of investment in
  that platform, makes it genuinely difficult for an outside analyst to assess whether it is keeping pace
  with newer digital-auction/marketplace competitors (private-sector e-auction platforms, generic B2B
  marketplaces, etc.) or accumulating technical debt. This should be flagged explicitly in the final
  report as an information gap that itself constitutes a risk signal, separate from any conclusion about
  the underlying reality.
