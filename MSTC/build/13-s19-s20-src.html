Restatements and shifting bases found

Three separate instances of the same metric on two bases
  1. Transacted value. An FY23 figure of about Rs 3,016 cr circulates under the same label as the FY25 and FY26 figures of Rs 80,000 cr to Rs 90,000 cr. That is a thirty-fold gap that no commodity cycle explains. The FY23 figure almost certainly covers only the principal-risk trading vertical while the later ones cover full auction volume. Joining them would produce a fictitious growth rate. This report does not join them.
  2. Revenue. FY26 is quoted as both Rs 369.66 cr and Rs 453.04 cr. Both are correct: the first is revenue from operations, the second total income including about Rs 83.38 cr of other income. The apparent contradiction that stalled several secondary analyses dissolves once the base is pinned.
  3. EBITDA. The company reports Q1 FY27 EBITDA of Rs 81.49 cr. The market data service reports Rs 57.73 cr for the same quarter. The difference is exactly the Rs 23.76 cr of other income. MSTC's own EBITDA definition includes treasury income. An investor comparing MSTC's stated EBITDA margin with a peer's operating EBITDA margin is comparing two different things, and MSTC comes out roughly 35 per cent better than it should.

The single sentence version

A mechanical screen sees a debt-free, cash-rich, 24 per cent return on equity government platform at 21.9 times with a 2.2 per cent yield and flags it as cheap quality. Sizing the platform above MSTC in the chain

GeM's FY26 gross merchandise value of Rs 5.03 lakh cr is 6.3 times MSTC's Rs 79,737 cr, and its cumulative Rs 18.4 lakh cr since 2016 is more than twenty years of MSTC volume at current run rates. Micro and small enterprises accounted for 47.1 per cent of GeM's FY26 volume across more than 51 lakh orders, and state government procurement on GeM grew 38.3 per cent.

The reframing this section normally produces, that a competition narrative is really a market-already-lost narrative, does not apply cleanly here, and saying so is more useful than forcing it. GeM is a buyer's marketplace; MSTC is a seller's auction house. The categories barely overlap today and no official statement of intent to extend GeM into disposal was found. What the comparison establishes is capability and precedent: the infrastructure to absorb MSTC's work exists, is already connected to every ministry, and charges nothing.

The more immediate competitive fact is narrower and better evidenced: Indian Railways runs roughly Rs 2.65 lakh cr a year entirely inside its own system. That is three times MSTC's whole platform volume, sitting permanently outside it, at one counterparty, because of a commission dispute two decades ago.

Where the official data and management's commentary disagree

Contradiction one, and it cuts in MSTC's favour

Management attributed the 36.65 per cent fall in first-half FY26 transacted value to softening scrap prices. Official trade data for the same year shows domestic scrap generation up 22 per cent to 32 million tonnes, consumption up 17 per cent to about 41 million tonnes, and scrap-based crude steel output up 16 per cent against 11 per cent for steel overall.

These are not inconsistent, since an ad valorem fee is geared to value and not to tonnage. But the framing conceals something useful. Physical activity on MSTC's platform was not falling. Only the rupees per tonne were. That sets a much lower bar for recovery than a volume problem would: MSTC does not need new mandates or more auctions to restore transacted value, it needs scrap prices to stop falling. Q1 FY27's Rs 23,814 cr, which annualises above the whole of FY26, suggests that has already begun. This is the most valuable forward-looking inference in the report and nobody appears to be modelling it.

Contradiction two, which is really a false pattern to avoid

GeM's gross merchandise value fell 7.4 per cent in FY26 and MSTC's fell 11.2 per cent in the same year. It is tempting to read one shared government slowdown into that. The officially stated causes do not support it: GeM's decline is attributed to lower order value from central public sector enterprises, a buyer-spend explanation, while MSTC's is attributed to scrap prices, a commodity explanation. Two different mechanisms, same direction, same year. This report records the coincidence and declines to make a causal claim.

Contradiction three, internal to MSTC

In FY26 MSTC's transacted value fell 11 per cent while its fee revenue rose 17 per cent and its reported profit fell 46 per cent. All three are true and all three point different ways. The profit fall is the FY25 disposal gain dropping out, and profit before exceptional items actually grew 22.84 per cent. The revenue-versus-volume gap is the take rate rising from about 31 to about 41 basis points on the record mineral block year.

Not one of those three headline numbers describes what happened to the business, and a reader who takes any of them at face value will reach the wrong conclusion. That is the clearest single illustration of why this company requires the filings that this report could not open.
