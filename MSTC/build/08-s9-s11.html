The single largest company-specific risk is not commercial

Contingent liabilities of about Rs 1,517 cr sit against a net worth of about Rs 900 cr. On the lowest net worth reading found they are more than twice it. Roughly Rs 943 cr is disputed tax, which in India can sit unresolved for a decade and then crystallise.

MSTC's entire market capitalisation is Rs 5,103 cr. An adverse outcome on even a third of the tax book would consume the balance sheet. This is not a tail risk that shows up in a screen, and it is the reason the credit rating is BBB+ on a debt-free company.

Regulatory dependencies

  • Competitive tendering is mandatory. The Central Vigilance Commission has required, from 01-Jan-2009, that even the selection of an e-auction service provider goes through competitive tender. There is no standing circular reserving this work for MSTC by name.
  • Mineral auctions run under the mining act and the 2015 auction rules. MSTC is nominated service provider for major mineral blocks and for minor blocks in five named states, which implies other states use other agencies.
  • Insolvency rules now channel liquidation asset sales elsewhere. From 01-Apr-2025 the insolvency regulator mandated exclusive use of the bank-consortium platform for liquidation asset sales.
  • Vehicle scrapping runs under the 2021 policy, with mandatory fitness tests for commercial vehicles over 15 years and passenger vehicles over 20, plus draft end-of-life vehicle management rules from 2024 tightening facility compliance.

Regulatory tailwinds

  • The Delhi-NCR replacement scheme cleared on 03-Jun-2026, with a Rs 9,585 cr outlay, Rs 5,041 cr of central support and Rs 1,601 cr of state tax concessions, covering about 1.91 lakh truck owners and 16,329 bus owners across four states, with a 5 per cent interest subsidy for five years and monthly fuel vouchers up to Rs 4,800.
  • Elimination of customs duty on critical-mineral waste and scrap, and a Rs 1,500 cr scheme rolled out in Oct-2025 for critical mineral recovery from secondary sources under the national critical mineral mission.
  • Record mineral block auction cadence, discussed in Sections 01 and 20.

Concentration risks

MSTC's customer concentration could not be quantified because no major-customer disclosure was reachable. What is known is that Coal India runs two separate mandates with MSTC and is plainly the anchor relationship, and that the current e-auction work order covers about 40 per cent of Coal India's work value and expires around Mar-2027. Single-counterparty renewal risk concentrated into one date in March 2027 is the sharpest identifiable risk on the calendar. There is no plant risk, no single-geography manufacturing risk and no supplier risk, because there is no physical business.

Quantifying the scrap price sensitivity, approximately

MSTC does not publish a sensitivity and this report cannot compute an exact one. But the arithmetic of an ad valorem fee gives a clean approximation: because the take rate is charged on value, a given percentage move in the average realised price of what is auctioned flows through to revenue roughly one for one, with no offsetting cost. Operating costs are largely fixed at roughly Rs 145 cr a year, so the flow-through to profit is amplified.

On FY26 numbers, a 10 per cent fall in average auction realisation, with volumes unchanged, would cut revenue by roughly Rs 33 cr to Rs 37 cr and operating profit by almost the same amount, roughly 15 per cent of operating EBIT. That is the real beta in this business, and it is invisible in a margin table because the margin barely moves.

The one genuine tailwind that is being under-read

Official trade data for FY26 shows domestic scrap generation up 22 per cent to 32 million tonnes, consumption up 17 per cent to about 41 million tonnes, and scrap-based crude steel output up 16 per cent against 11 per cent for steel overall. Physical scrap activity grew strongly in exactly the year MSTC's transacted value fell. Management's own framing, that the problem was prices, concedes that activity was not the issue.

That matters for forecasting because it sets a lower bar than the headline suggests. MSTC does not need to win new mandates or grow volumes to recover its transacted value. It needs scrap prices to stop falling. Q1 FY27's transacted value of Rs 23,814 cr, which annualises above the whole of FY26, is consistent with that already happening.
