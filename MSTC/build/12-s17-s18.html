Decomposing the spend

An itemised split of growth, maintenance and non-operating capital expenditure is not obtainable, because the fixed asset note could not be read. What can be established is bounding.

  • Total capital spending is Rs 22.26 cr a year, which is 0.4 per cent of market capitalisation and 6 per cent of revenue. Whatever the split, none of the buckets can be large.
  • No non-operating capital expenditure was identified. No head office purchase, no land, no aircraft, no guest house. Given how small the total is, there is no room to hide one. On the specific trap of a single large discretionary asset buried inside a multi-year total, MSTC appears clean by arithmetic rather than by disclosure.
  • The replacement ratio is 2.0 times depreciation, which is comfortably above the 1.0 threshold below which a productive asset base is being consumed faster than it is renewed. On this measure MSTC is over-investing relative to its accounting consumption, not under-investing.
The replacement ratio looks reassuring and may be measuring the wrong thing

For an asset-light platform business, depreciation is a poor benchmark for technology adequacy, because a software platform can be fully depreciated and still be perfectly adequate, or be carried at cost and be a decade obsolete. What matters is whether the portal a scrap trader logs into is competitive.

Rs 22 cr a year, against Rs 225 cr of operating profit and a platform that clears Rs 80,000 cr of transactions, is a thin technology budget in absolute terms. No disclosure of platform age, uptime, cybersecurity posture or software capitalisation exists in anything reachable for this report, and the scuttlebutt pass that would have tested it from the bidder side could not be run. This is recorded as accumulating technical debt that is possible but unproven, not as a finding.

Aggregate incremental return, and why it cannot be computed

The standard calculation, change in earnings before interest and tax divided by capital deployed over the period, requires a consistent earnings series across FY24 to FY26. MSTC does not have one: the trading business was wound down, a wholly owned subsidiary was sold, and the revenue line fell from Rs 721 cr to Rs 316 cr on presentation. Any incremental return figure computed across that break would be an artefact. It is recorded as not computable rather than estimated.

What can be said qualitatively is sharper. The operating business needs essentially no capital. Operating profit rose from Rs 188.46 cr in FY25 to Rs 246.33 cr in FY26 while capital expenditure was Rs 22.26 cr. On any reading, the incremental return on capital deployed in the platform is very high and far above any plausible cost of equity.

The capital allocation verdict

MSTC has no reinvestment outlet at its incremental return, and it has behaved accordingly. The platform absorbs about Rs 22 cr a year and cannot absorb more. There is no plant to build, no acquisition pipeline, and no way to buy market share in a business allocated by tender.

Faced with Rs 320 cr of subsidiary sale proceeds in FY25, management distributed essentially all of it as dividend: Rs 45.50 per share, or Rs 320.3 cr against proceeds of Rs 320 cr. For a company with nowhere to compound, that was the right decision, and it is the strongest single piece of evidence in this report that capital allocation here is rational.

Two qualifications. First, the gap between the return on the platform and the return on the roughly Rs 900 cr of shareholders' funds parked in receivables and deposits is enormous, and it is the strongest available argument for a still higher payout or a buyback, which MSTC has never done. Second, the announced travel business is the first proposal in years to deploy capital into something new, and it is precisely the kind of decision that ends a good distribution record. Watch what MSTC does with the next windfall, not what it did with the last one.

Mandate register and execution track record

MSTC does not report an order book. A register of named mandates is more informative for a tender-driven business anyway, and it is where the bear case lives.

Confirmed: there is no order book, and the reason is structural

No source reached for this report uses order book or order inflow language for MSTC. Every operating metric it discloses is a flow: transacted value over a period, or a count of auction events. That is appropriate. A commission earned on transactions as they occur does not create contracted future revenue that can be presented as backlog, and MSTC does not manufacture one for investor communication.

The consequence for an analyst is that book to bill cannot be computed, and where order announcements are filed without a stated consideration, as is usual here, neither can any inflow series. This is a genuine limitation, not an oversight.

Mandates lost or migrated away

This is the strongest evidence in the report, in either direction

Indian Railways. A pilot ran on Southern Railway in 2006-07 using MSTC's software. Railways then decided to build its own e-auction capability in house after MSTC sought roughly 1.5 to 2 per cent of sale proceeds. It today runs scrap auctions and roughly Rs 2.65 lakh cr a year of procurement through IREPS. The decision has never been reversed. One of India's largest scrap generators demonstrated that the commission was worth more than the platform.

The bank asset auction platform. The Indian Banks' Association engaged MSTC to build a common e-auction platform, launched by the Finance Minister on 28-Dec-2019. It is now described as owned and managed by a consortium of twelve public sector banks and operated by one of them, with a rebranded portal launched on 03-Jan-2025. From 01-Apr-2025 the insolvency regulator mandated exclusive use of that platform for liquidation asset sales. Whether MSTC retains any residual technology or hosting fee could not be established, and that distinction is financially material.

Coal India, partially. Not a loss, but a documented dilution: the current work order covers about 40 per cent of the estimated work value, so a competitor holds the majority.

GeM absorption. No specific evidence found either way. It remains an open risk rather than a confirmed loss, and this report does not claim otherwise.

Execution: core versus new

Core auction and procurement · reliable

MSTC has been the venue of record for commercial coal block auctions across rounds numbering into the mid-teens, has renewed Coal India twice within eleven months, and executed a Rs 2,259.52 cr state land sale in a single quarter. It won four new categories between Sep-2025 and Mar-2026.

Where execution data exists, it is about the demand for what is being auctioned rather than about MSTC. In the 15th coal block round only 8 of 23 blocks drew bids, 21 bids from 16 companies. That is a market outcome, not a platform failure, but it caps MSTC's fee just the same.

New verticals · unproven or unmeasurable

The vehicle scrapping joint venture has three to nine facilities depending on source, a stated ambition of one million vehicles of capacity by FY27, and no disclosed throughput at all after roughly eight years.

The travel business is a stated plan with an exclusive partner, no capital figure, no timeline and no launch confirmation. The producer-responsibility credit platform has an approval and no launch confirmation. No consumer marketplace branding was found.

The split is stark: MSTC executes reliably in the business it has always been in, and has published no completed delivery in any new vertical.

Growth strategy, reconstructed

A verbatim numbered strategy from management could not be obtained, because the annual report and investor presentations were unreachable. What follows is reconstructed from individual announcements and is labelled as such.

  1. Win and retain large recurring government auction mandates. Two Coal India mandates inside eleven months. Extends demonstrated capability
  2. Extend the same auction skill to new asset classes: land, liquor licences, gold quota, pollution credits. Natural adjacency
  3. Scale the vehicle scrapping joint venture. Requires physical dismantling, environmental compliance and metal processing, none of which is MSTC's historical competence. Partly new capability
  4. Exit the legacy trading business. Completed in Q1 FY27. A subtraction, not a growth strand, but a good one. Delivered
  5. Enter a travel business. Nothing in MSTC's history explains it, no capability has been hired for it that this report could identify, and it has never been explained on a call. Unexplained

Strands one, two and four belong in a forecast. Strand three belongs in an option bucket until a throughput number is published. Strand five belongs in the risk section, and it is in Section 15.
