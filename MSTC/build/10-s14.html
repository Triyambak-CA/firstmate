Section 14

Valuation

Reverse discounted cash flow first, because the only honest starting question is what the current price already assumes. Then the forward case, then a sum of the parts that produces an uncomfortably precise answer.

Choosing the right multiple, and the trap underneath it

For an asset-light fee platform the natural primary multiple is enterprise value to operating profit, because it strips the treasury book and looks only at the franchise. For MSTC that choice immediately runs into the question everything else in this report has been building towards: what is the enterprise value?

The cash on MSTC's balance sheet is largely not shareholders' cash

Cash and investments are roughly Rs 1,148 cr to Rs 1,360 cr. Debt is Rs 145 cr. A screen nets those and reports about Rs 1,035 cr of net cash, a fifth of the market capitalisation, and concludes MSTC is cheap.

But non-debt liabilities are roughly Rs 1,073 cr to Rs 1,216 cr, and the balance sheet identity holds: net worth of about Rs 900 cr is almost exactly matched by non-cash assets of about Rs 950 cr less debt. The cash pile and the deposit liabilities very nearly cancel. That is the arithmetic signature of a float business holding bidders' earnest money, and MSTC's own auction terms confirm that bidders post non-interest-bearing deposits before they can participate.

The exact earnest money liability line could not be read, so neither extreme can be proved. This report therefore values MSTC on both enterprise values and lets the reader see the spread. It also states plainly which one it believes: given the near-exact offset, the float-adjusted case, in which shareholder surplus cash is close to zero, is the more probable of the two.

Translate that into transacted value, and the assumption stops sounding modest

MSTC's realised take rate is compressing, not expanding, so fee growth has to come from transacted value. Growing the fee base 5.1 to 6.7 times over a decade therefore requires transacted value to rise from Rs 79,737 cr to somewhere between Rs 4.0 lakh cr and Rs 5.4 lakh cr.

Rs 5.4 lakh cr is GeM's entire gross merchandise value in FY25, across all of Indian government procurement. Rs 4.0 lakh cr is GeM's FY24. At today's price, on the enterprise value this report considers more likely, MSTC is being asked to become as large as GeM is now, and to hold its take rate while doing it.

That is not impossible. It is, however, a great deal more demanding than "10 per cent growth forever," and it is the honest statement of what a buyer at Rs 728 is underwriting.

What the market is actually paying for the two things it cannot see

Identifiable value comes to Rs 4,628 cr, or Rs 657 a share. The market capitalisation at Rs 728.20 on 7.04 cr shares is Rs 5,126 cr. The gap is Rs 498 cr, almost exactly 10 per cent of the company.

So at today's price, the market is simultaneously paying about Rs 500 cr for a 50:50 joint venture whose revenue, profit and vehicle throughput have never been disclosed, and assigning a probability-weighted cost of zero to Rs 1,517 cr of contingent claims that exceed net worth.

Neither assumption is absurd on its own. Holding both at once is the position an investor takes at Rs 728, and it deserves to be stated out loud rather than buried in a multiple.
