MSTC Limited

NSE: MSTCLTD · BSE: 542597 · 21-Sep-2026 · Data as of Q1 FY27 (Jun-2026)

A 289-person Government of India toll booth on the state's sell side. MSTC runs the e-auction platforms on which ministries, public sector undertakings and state governments dispose of scrap, coal, mineral blocks, seized goods and surplus land, and takes roughly 40 basis points of whatever clears. It owns almost no assets, employs almost nobody, and controls almost none of its own volume.

Executive summary

One page. Read the two flags below before anything else, because one of them governs how much weight every number in this report can carry.

Source access was restricted while this report was written

Every primary filing source was refused by the research environment's network policy on 21-Sep-2026: the exchanges, the company's own investor pages, the government press and disinvestment sites, and the usual data portals. The web-search allowance was also exhausted during the research pass.

As a result, the annual report, the notes to accounts, the cash flow statement, the segment note, the related party schedule, the auditor's report and the shareholding filings were never opened directly. What follows is built from a licensed market data feed, dated news-wire copy, and credit rating commentary quoted at second hand. Items that could not be verified are marked as such throughout, and the report says where a conclusion is provisional. Treat this as a well-evidenced working thesis, not an audited one.

Earnings quality: 4 out of 10

Provisional. Marked down for contingent liabilities at roughly 1.7 times net worth, operating cash flow at 23 per cent of reported profit in FY26, and a profit line in which roughly a third of pre-tax income is interest earned on money that belongs to bidders. Marked up for a clean dividend trail that reconciles exactly to the cash flow statement and for no evidence of aggressive capitalisation.

Guidance credibility: not computable, and that is the finding

MSTC has held quarterly earnings calls since roughly Nov-2022, hosted by a broker. Across the available record, only two forward, quantifiable commitments could be identified, and both are still open. There is no meaningful history of the company setting a number and being measured against it. A scorecard cannot be built from nothing, and the absence itself belongs in the governance column, not in the growth column. Section 08 scores the substitutes instead.

The three things that matter

Positive 1 · The toll booth itself is exceptional

289 employees produced about Rs 225 cr of operating profit in the twelve months to Jun-2026 on Rs 386 cr of fee revenue, a 58 per cent operating margin, with capital expenditure of Rs 22 cr a year. There is almost no capital in the operating business, so the return on the capital that is actually at work approaches infinity. Very few businesses in India look like this.

Positive 2 · The volume drivers are genuinely accelerating on official data

Mineral blocks auctioned went from about 90 a year across FY22 to FY25 to a record 212 in FY26. All-India coal production hit an all-time high of 1,047.6 million tonnes in FY25 with an FY26 target of 1,150.39 million tonnes. Domestic ferrous scrap generation rose 22 per cent to 32 million tonnes in FY26. On 03-Jun-2026 the Union Cabinet cleared a Rs 9,585 cr Delhi-NCR vehicle replacement scheme covering about 2.07 lakh vehicle owners.

Positive 3 · FY26's weakness was a price effect, not an activity effect

Management blamed the 36.65 per cent first-half FY26 fall in transacted value on softening scrap prices. Official trade data for the same year shows scrap volumes up 22 per cent and scrap-based steel output up 16 per cent. Physical activity grew; only the rupee value per tonne fell. That is a mean-reverting problem, and a price normalisation alone restores growth without needing any volume help. Q1 FY27 already shows the turn.

Risk 1 · Two of the biggest customers already built their own road

Indian Railways piloted MSTC's software in 2006-07, balked at a proposed 1.5 to 2 per cent commission, and built IREPS in house. It still runs it. The common bank asset auction platform MSTC originally built and the Finance Minister launched on 28-Dec-2019 is now owned and managed by PSB Alliance, with a rebranded portal launched on 03-Jan-2025. Coal India's current e-auction work order, dated 21-Mar-2025, covers only about 40 per cent of the work value. These are not hypotheticals. They are the base rate.

Risk 2 · FY26's revenue growth was mix, not franchise

Transacted value fell about 11 per cent in FY26, from roughly Rs 89,823 cr to Rs 79,737 cr. Fee revenue rose 18.9 per cent. The only way both are true is that the realised take rate jumped, from roughly 31 basis points to roughly 41. The most likely cause is the record mineral-block year, which carries richer economics than scrap. In Q1 FY27 the rate fell back to about 37.6 basis points. Growth bought with a policy-driven mix spike is not the same asset as growth bought with share.

Risk 3 · The balance sheet is not the fortress the screen says it is

Contingent liabilities of about Rs 1,517 cr as at 31-Mar-2025, of which roughly Rs 943 cr is disputed tax, sit against a net worth of roughly Rs 900 cr. Cash and investments of roughly Rs 1,150 cr to Rs 1,360 cr are almost exactly matched by non-debt liabilities of roughly Rs 1,073 cr to Rs 1,216 cr, which is the signature of a float business holding other people's deposits. Despite being effectively debt free, MSTC's credit ratings are CARE BBB+ and Acuite A+, which is the rating agencies telling you what they think of the litigation and the legacy receivables.

This is a bet on the Indian state continuing to outsource its own asset disposal to a 289-person toll booth, priced as if that mandate were permanent and about 10 per cent perpetual compounding were automatic, and it breaks if Coal India's March-2027 renewal goes the way Indian Railways and the bank auction platform already went.
