# Section 1 - Industry and Sector: Government e-Procurement / e-Auction in India

Compiled 21-Sep-2026. All figures sourced and dated; contradictions flagged explicitly rather than reconciled by guesswork.

## 1.1 THE CRITICAL COMPARISON: GeM GMV vs MSTC GMV

GeM (Government e Marketplace, gem.gov.in) is the dominant government digital-procurement platform in India by an order of magnitude versus MSTC. This is the single most important structural fact for this sector.

**GeM annual GMV (Gross Merchandise Value), official PIB figures:**

| FY | GeM GMV | YoY | Source |
|---|---|---|---|
| FY22 (2021-22) | ~Rs 1,01,000 crore (crossed Rs 1 lakh crore) | +160% | PIB explainer doc, static.pib.gov.in/.../doc20226361301.pdf (Jun-2022) |
| FY23 (2022-23) | Rs 2,01,000 crore (~Rs 2.01 lakh crore) | ~+99% | Business Standard, 29-Mar-2024, citing GeM data |
| FY24 (2023-24) | Rs 4,00,000 crore (Rs 4 lakh crore) | ~+99% (doubled) | PIB PRID=2016631, "GeM crosses Rs 4 Lakh Crore in GMV at the end of this Fiscal Year, doubles business in a year" |
| FY25 (2024-25) | Rs 5,40,000 crore (Rs 5.4 lakh crore) | +35% | PIB PRID=2154136, "GeM 9th Foundation Day; records Rs 5.4 lakh crore GMV in FY 2024-25" |
| FY26 (2025-26) | Rs 5,03,000 crore (Rs 5.03 lakh crore) | **-7.4%** (DECLINE) | Zeebiz/Business Standard, citing Mines/Commerce data, reporting decline "primarily on account of lower order value from CPSEs" |

Cumulative GeM GMV since 2016 inception, as of FY26 close: **Rs 18.4 lakh crore** (PIB PRID=2249335, "GeM Achieves Rs 18.4 Lakh Crore GMV, Emerges as Key Digital Public Procurement Platform"; also IBEF citing same, US$197.72 billion equivalent). This cumulative figure roughly reconciles with the annual series above (sum FY22-FY26 plus FY17-21 residual approx Rs 17.4-18.4 lakh crore) - internally consistent.

FY26 services vs product split (from FY25 data, most granular available): services Rs 2.54 lakh crore (62%), products Rs 1.55 lakh crore (38%) of Rs 4.09 lakh crore measured at the 23-Jan-2025 checkpoint (PIB PRID=2095901).

FY26 MSE/state detail: MSEs accounted for 47.1% of FY26 GMV (Rs 2.36 lakh crore across >51 lakh orders to >11 lakh registered MSEs); state-government procurement on GeM grew 38.3% in FY26 (Lokmat Times / DD News, citing GeM data). **FY26 is the first year GeM's GMV has declined year-on-year** in the searched record - flagged as a notable inflection, driver stated as lower CPSE order value, not lower state/MSE activity (which grew).

|---|---|---|
| FY23 (2022-23), marketing+e-commerce verticals | Rs 3,015.90 crore (+120% YoY, vs ~Rs 1,371 cr implied FY22) | Wikipedia/MSTC citing company data |

| H1 FY26 (Apr-Sep 2025) | Rs 30,167 crore (Rs 301.67 billion), reported as **-36.65% YoY**, "primarily due to a softening trend in scrap prices" | Company commentary via financial media |

**FLAG - unresolved data inconsistency:** the FY23 figure (~Rs 3,016 crore) is roughly 30x smaller than the FY25/FY26 figures (~Rs 80,000-90,000 crore) for what multiple sources label the same metric ("value of goods transacted through e-commerce and marketing verticals"). This is too large a jump to be organic growth (scrap/commodity prices did not rise 30x) and likely reflects either (a) a change in what MSTC discloses as "value of goods transacted" between FY23 and FY25 filings (e.g., FY23 figure may cover only the principal-risk "trading" vertical, while FY25/26 figures cover the full e-auction/e-commerce GMV including coal and mineral-block auctions where MSTC only earns a fee), or (b) a reporting/units error in one of the secondary sources. Could not verify from primary MSTC annual report PDF (mstcindia.co.in is blocked from this environment's egress; screener.in also blocked). **Treat the FY25/FY26 ~Rs 80,000-90,000 crore range as the more decision-relevant "true" e-auction GMV figure** (it is corroborated across three separate periods - H1FY25, FY25 full year, 9M FY26, FY26 full year - with internally consistent half-year/full-year math), but this needs primary-source confirmation before use in any valuation model.

**Sizing conclusion:** Using the higher, more-corroborated MSTC GMV figure, GeM's FY26 GMV (Rs 5.03 lakh crore) is **roughly 6.3x larger** than MSTC's FY26 transacted value (Rs 79,737 crore, i.e. Rs 0.80 lakh crore). GeM is not currently active in MSTC's core categories (scrap, minerals, coal, seized/confiscated goods disposal) - GeM is a buy-side procurement marketplace (ministries/PSUs buying goods and services), while MSTC is predominantly a sell-side disposal/auction platform (government/PSU entities selling surplus, scrap, minerals, seized assets). The overlap is currently limited, but GeM's stated ambition and scale (it already handles services procurement, which is adjacent territory) make it the platform most likely to expand into MSTC's turf if government policy chose to consolidate. NOT FOUND: any official statement of intent for GeM to enter physical-asset disposal/e-auction of scrap or minerals.

## 1.2 Other official/quasi-official e-procurement platforms

- **CPPP (Central Public Procurement Portal, eprocure.gov.in):** developed and operated by NIC (National Informatics Centre) under Ministry of Finance; the mandated publication point for tender notices by central ministries/departments/attached offices (buy-side tendering, not auction). Distinct mandate from MSTC (tendering for purchases vs. auction for disposals), so direct competitive overlap is limited, but it is evidence that government has built free, in-house alternatives to fee-charging platforms for the *procurement* (buy) side. NOT FOUND: CPPP's own GMV/transaction-value figures in this research pass.
- **IREPS (Indian Railways e-Procurement System, ireps.gov.in):** built and operated by CRIS (Centre for Railway Information Systems), in-house to Indian Railways, since ~2009. Railways spends roughly **Rs 2.65 lakh crore/year** on rolling stock, signalling, track, stations, electrification and services (bidindia.co.in, sourced to Railways budget data - treat as approximate/secondary, not a primary Railways figure). Under GFR rules, IREPS tenders must also be mirrored on CPPP for visibility, but actual bidding stays on IREPS. This is a clean example of a large government buyer building and keeping its own captive e-procurement system rather than using GeM or a PSU platform like MSTC - i.e., a structural centrifugal force against any single-platform consolidation (including in MSTC's favour).
- **Coal India's own auction infrastructure:** coal.nic.in hosts a "nominated-authority/auction-portal" page and a separate coaljunction.in ("CIL MJ Auction") portal appears to exist alongside MSTC's coal e-auction operations (mstcecommerce.com/coalauctionhome). MSTC's most recent CIL contract (won ~10-Mar-2025, formalised ~21-Mar-2025) is a **2-year e-auction services work order covering 166 auction events and only 40% of the estimated work value** (Business Upturn, 21-Mar-2025) - i.e., CIL split the coal e-auction mandate across more than one service provider rather than giving MSTC the whole contract. This is an important, probably under-appreciated structural fact: **MSTC is not the sole coal e-auction vendor to Coal India**, and the relationship is tender-based and renewable, not a standing exclusive mandate. NOT FOUND: identity of the counterparty holding the other ~60% of CIL's e-auction work, or coaljunction.in's ownership/operator - flag for follow-up.
- **Mineral block e-auctions (MMDR Act 1957 + Mineral (Auction) Rules 2015):** MSTC's website states it is the "nominated service provider for all major mineral blocks and minor mineral blocks of a few states, namely Assam, Chhattisgarh, Jharkhand, Rajasthan and Uttar Pradesh" - implying other states/blocks may use different auction agencies. Ministry of Mines auction summary portal (mines.gov.in/webportal/content/mineral-auction-summary) could not be fetched directly (egress-blocked); volume data below is from secondary press coverage of Ministry announcements. **FY22-FY25 cumulative: 364 mineral blocks auctioned (~90/year average).** **FY26: record 212 blocks auctioned** (per newkerala.com/indiacsr.in, citing Mines Ministry, reporting date ~19-Mar-2026), including 22 critical/strategic mineral blocks; a slightly earlier Mines Ministry tweet/DD News item (19-Mar-2026) cites "200 blocks" (123 Mining Lease + 77 Composite Licence) as of that date - the 212 figure appears to be the final full-FY26 count, the 200 figure an in-year checkpoint. Operationalisation (blocks actually producing) remains the bottleneck: only 36 blocks (28 greenfield + 8 brownfield) were operationalised in FY26 alone, vs a cumulative 58 blocks (20 greenfield + 38 brownfield) operationalised across the entire FY16-FY25 period - i.e., operationalisation is now accelerating faster than in the first decade of the auction regime, a volume tailwind for MSTC's per-event/percentage-fee model if MSTC retains this nominated role.
- **eBKray / BAANKNET / IBAPI (bank NPA and insolvency-liquidation asset auctions) - IMPORTANT CORRECTION to task brief:** research indicates **eBKray was NOT built by MSTC.** e-BKray (launched 28-Feb-2019) is owned and operated by **PSB Alliance Private Limited**, a company jointly owned by the 12 public-sector banks - not MSTC, and not IBA directly either (business-standard.com, 10-Jan-2025; psballiance.com). Effective 1-Apr-2025, **IBBI mandated exclusive use of eBKray for listing all unsold assets in ongoing insolvency-liquidation cases** (Business Standard, 10-Jan-2025). Separately, **BAANKNET** (launched 3-Jan-2025) is a revamped, centralised e-auction portal, also PSB-Alliance/IBA-linked, now used by all 12 PSU banks plus IBBI for listing and auctioning bank NPA properties; it reportedly lifted auction success rates to 14.7% from 9% under the prior fragmented system. **MSTC operates a separate, MSTC-owned bank-property e-auction platform** (mstcecommerce.com/auctionhome/ibapi), which is one of the platforms linked from the IBAPI aggregator site (ibapi.in, an IBA/Department of Financial Services initiative that itself is a listings aggregator, not a bidding venue - bids redirect out to MSTC or eBKray). Coverage suggests MSTC's own platform continues to serve **private-sector banks** (HDFC, ICICI, Axis) for their NPA-property auctions, while **PSU banks are consolidating onto the bank-owned BAANKNET/eBKray stack**. **This is a genuine, and probably underweighted, competitive-erosion risk for MSTC in the bank-asset-auction vertical**: the 12 PSU banks that dominate India's NPA volumes now own and control their own platform (PSB Alliance) rather than using a Ministry-of-Steel-controlled PSU intermediary. NOT FOUND: MSTC's actual revenue contribution from the bank-NPA-auction vertical (to size the exposure), or a direct company statement on BAANKNET's impact.
- **National Land Monetisation Corporation (NLMC):** 100%-GoI-owned company under the Department of Public Enterprises (DPE), Ministry of Finance, tasked with monetising surplus CPSE/government land and building assets. Its 21st Board Meeting (18-Sep-2026) recommended monetisation proposals worth **>Rs 5,000 crore** of surplus land/building assets (multiple near-identical press items, e.g. Hello Entrepreneurs/Impressive Times, 18/19-Sep-2026). NOT FOUND: whether NLMC routes actual disposal/auction execution through MSTC's e-auction platform, through its own mechanism, or through open real-estate brokerage/tender - this is a real gap and matters because land monetisation is exactly the kind of "government asset disposal" mandate that could organically belong to MSTC but may instead be built as a parallel, NLMC-controlled process.

## 1.3 Vehicle Scrappage Policy / RVSF ecosystem (MSTC-Mahindra JV "CERO" operates here)

- Regulatory base: Vehicle Scrapping Policy, 2021 (MoRTH), mandatory fitness tests for commercial vehicles >15 years and passenger vehicles >20 years; Ministry of Environment, Forest and Climate Change's **Draft End-of-Life Vehicles (Management) Rules, 2024** adds environmental/safety compliance requirements for Registered Vehicle Scrapping Facilities (RVSFs).
- **RVSF count: approximately 80-90 RVSFs hold provisional or final registration nationally as of early 2026** (nationalrecycling.in blog, citing industry tracking - secondary source, not an official MoRTH count; treat as directional, NOT an official figure). This is set against an estimated **~1.5 crore (15 million) end-of-life vehicles requiring disposal over the next 3-5 years** - i.e., registered-facility capacity is dramatically under-built relative to the addressable ELV pool. Geographic clustering is heavy around Delhi-NCR, Mumbai Metropolitan Region, Pune, Ahmedabad, Chennai and Bengaluru; coverage elsewhere remains sparse.
- **CERO (Mahindra MSTC Recycling Pvt Ltd, the MSTC-Mahindra JV, est. Jan-2018 at Greater Noida):** as of research, CERO operates **9 scrapping centres serving 36 cities** (search-derived, undated snapshot circa 2025-26 - confirm against latest company IR material). CERO's leadership (Sumit Issar, in an Autocar Professional interview) has stated an aim to **expand capacity to dismantle 1 million ELVs by FY 2026-27** - this is a **company/management target, not an achieved or audited figure; label as directional/aspirational only.** Renault India has partnered with CERO to support scrappage-policy compliance for its customers (autorecyclingworld.com). NOT FOUND: CERO's actual revenue, vehicles-scrapped-to-date count, or profitability contribution to MSTC's consolidated results in this research pass - a material gap given CERO is one of MSTC's named growth engines.

## 1.4 Cycle position and demand drivers

- MSTC's own H1 FY26 commentary attributes a **36.65% YoY decline** in transacted value explicitly to "a softening trend in scrap prices" - i.e., MSTC's GMV (and hence fee income, being ad-valorem) is **directly geared to metal/commodity scrap prices**, not just to physical auction volumes. This is a genuine commodity-price-cyclical exposure embedded in what looks like an asset-light "platform" business.
- Coal volumes (a major MSTC auction category) are on a rising government-targeted trajectory: **India's total coal production hit an all-time high of 1,047.6 million tonnes in FY25**, with the Ministry of Coal targeting **1,150.39 million tonnes in FY26 (~+10% YoY)** (Telangana Today/CareEdge, citing Ministry of Coal). **Coal India Limited (CIL) itself produced 781.1 MT in FY25 (missing its own target by ~7%)**, with an FY26 target variously reported as 868 MT or 875 MT (~76% of the all-India target) across sources - flag the range as unreconciled between sources. Rising coal production is a volume tailwind for e-auction GMV in the coal category, but MSTC's claim on that GMV is now only partial (see 1.2 above - CIL's FY25 contract covered 40% of estimated work value only).
- Mineral block auction cadence is **accelerating and at a record high in FY26** (212 blocks vs ~90/year average FY22-FY25), a clear structural tailwind if MSTC retains nominated-provider status in the relevant states.
- Steel scrap (largest single MSTC auction category by volume, alongside coal/minerals): **India's ferrous scrap consumption rose 17% YoY to a record ~41 million tonnes in FY26** (from ~35 Mt in FY25), with **domestic scrap generation up 22% YoY to 32 Mt**, reducing import dependence; scrap-based crude steel production rose 16% YoY to 38 Mt, outpacing the 11% growth in overall crude steel production (SteelOrbis, citing trade data). **Ferrous scrap imports fell ~20% in FY26** on cheaper domestic alternatives. Net read: rising domestic scrap *generation* is a volume tailwind for MSTC's scrap e-auction business, even as scrap *prices* (and hence ad-valorem fee income per tonne) may be softening per MSTC's own H1 FY26 commentary - i.e., volume and price drivers may be pulling in opposite directions on MSTC's revenue at the same time. This tension (rising physical volumes, falling prices) is a key thing to track each quarter.
- Disinvestment/asset-monetisation pipeline (a secondary demand driver, mainly for MSTC's role in PSU-linked land/surplus-asset and equity-adjacent transactions - see notes-08 for full detail): **FY27 combined disinvestment + asset-monetisation target Rs 80,000 crore**, up sharply from an FY26 revised outcome of roughly **Rs 34,000 crore against an original Rs 47,000 crore budget target** - i.e., FY26 execution missed budget materially, and FY27's target implies a large step-up that is not guaranteed to land (label as a budget target, not a committed outcome).

## 1.5 Structural shifts

1. **GeM's GMV declined in FY26** (first observed decline in this research's data series) after several years of very rapid (99-160%) YoY growth - suggests GeM's easy/organic growth phase (onboarding new categories, new buyers) may be maturing, with CPSE order value specifically cited as the FY26 drag. Whether this changes GeM's appetite to expand into adjacent categories (like disposal/auction, MSTC's turf) is unclear - NOT FOUND.
2. **Ministries/PSUs increasingly build and keep their own captive procurement portals** rather than consolidating onto GeM or MSTC (IREPS for Railways being the clearest example, handling ~Rs 2.65 lakh crore/year entirely outside GeM/MSTC). This fragmentation cuts against any single platform's network-effect moat, MSTC's included.
3. **Bank-owned platforms (PSB Alliance's eBKray/BAANKNET) are consolidating the PSU bank NPA-auction vertical away from third-party intermediaries**, a direct and fairly recent (2025) structural loss of addressable market for MSTC in that specific category, even as MSTC retains private-bank NPA-auction business.
4. **Coal India has moved to a split/multi-vendor e-auction contracting model** (MSTC covering ~40% of estimated FY25-27 contract value, not 100%), formalising what was previously assumed (by the task brief) to be closer to an exclusive MSTC arrangement. Contestability is real and current, not hypothetical.
5. Government asset-monetisation infrastructure is being built as **new, purpose-specific vehicles** (NLMC for land, NMP 2.0 for infrastructure concessions) rather than being centralised through MSTC, suggesting government's default instinct is to create fresh single-purpose institutions rather than route new asset classes through the existing MSTC platform.

## 1.6 Margin structure - who captures value

- MSTC's own disclosed **transaction/commission rate is approximately 0.4% of GMV** (i.e., roughly 40 basis points), per financial-media coverage of company disclosures - this is the ad-valorem fee MSTC earns on the value of goods/assets transacted through its e-auction platform. NOT FOUND: whether this 0.4% is a blended average across all categories (coal, minerals, scrap, bank NPA, land) or specific to one category - category-level fee rates were not found in this research pass and likely vary (e.g., high-value mineral-block auctions plausibly carry a lower ad-valorem rate or flat/tiered fee structure vs small-ticket scrap lots).
- Coal e-auction specific charges found (from MSTC's own coal-auction bidder documentation): a flat **document-processing fee of Rs 2,360 (Rs 2,000 + 18% GST)** and an **EMD (earnest money deposit) of Rs 200/tonne across grades** - these are upfront/security charges on bidders, not MSTC's commission per se, and do not by themselves reveal MSTC's percentage take from CIL. NOT FOUND: the specific commission/service-charge percentage MSTC earns from Coal India under the current 2-year contract.
- **GeM's fee model is different and largely free/low-cost to buyers and sellers** by design (it is a government-owned, non-profit-motive marketplace funded through the Ministry of Commerce rather than a fee-maximising platform) - this is a structural reason GeM's growth has not directly cannibalised MSTC's disposal-side fee business to date, but also caps how MSTC could compete if GeM ever entered the disposal/auction space on a similarly low-fee basis.
- Listed exchange/platform comparables (IEX, MCX, CDSL, BSE - see notes-07) all monetise via basis-point transaction/turnover charges or annual fees on assets under custody, and post EBITDA margins materially higher than MSTC's (MSTC's FY26 EBITDA margin, using the most internally consistent figures - Rs 307.49 crore EBITDA on Rs 453.04 crore total income - works out to roughly 68%, though this specific EBITDA figure carries its own sourcing caveat, see notes-08). If confirmed, an ~68% EBITDA margin would actually be comparable to or better than several listed exchange peers, which is notable given MSTC trades at a much lower earnings multiple (see notes-07) - possibly because of the FY26 profit decline, PSU-discount, and the commodity-price-cyclicality of its fee base, none of which apply to pure post-trade/registry businesses like CDSL/CAMS/KFin.

## 1.7 Regulatory architecture - is MSTC's revenue contestable?

- **CVC (Central Vigilance Commission) mandated, effective 1-Jan-2009, that even the selection of e-tendering/e-procurement service providers by government bodies must go through a formal competitive tender process** - i.e., there is no standing CVC/DoE circular that reserves e-auction service-provider work for MSTC by name or by category. (Source: secondary summary citing CVC guideline history; primary CVC circular text not directly retrieved in this pass - NOT FOUND as primary document, treat as high-confidence secondary citation only.)
- In practice, MSTC holds a mix of (a) **administrative/nomination-based mandates** in specific instances (e.g., its own website states it is the nominated service provider for major/minor mineral-block auctions in five named states: Assam, Chhattisgarh, Jharkhand, Rajasthan, Uttar Pradesh) and (b) **competitively tendered, time-bound work orders** (the Coal India 2-year, 40%-of-value contract won ~Mar-2025 being the clearest recent example, implying a competitive multi-vendor process for at least part of CIL's business).
- **Conclusion: MSTC's revenue base is contestable in principle and, in at least the Coal India case, demonstrably contested in practice (split-vendor award).** There is no evidence found of a blanket government policy mandating all ministries/PSUs to route e-auction/disposal work through MSTC specifically. This is an important valuation input: MSTC's moat, where it exists, appears to rest on incumbency, government ownership/trust, in-house platform IP, and administrative convenience (nomination) rather than on a codified regulatory monopoly - and at least one major client (Coal India) has already introduced competitive multi-sourcing. NOT FOUND: a comprehensive list of which of MSTC's client relationships are nomination-based vs competitively tendered - this would materially sharpen the contestability assessment and is a priority follow-up item.
