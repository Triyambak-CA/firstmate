Earnings quality: 4 out of 10

Provisional. Marked down for contingent liabilities at roughly 1.7 times net worth, operating cash flow at 23 per cent of reported profit in FY26, and a profit line in which roughly a third of pre-tax income is interest earned on money that belongs to bidders. Marked up for a clean dividend trail that reconciles exactly to the cash flow statement and for no evidence of aggressive capitalisation.

Guidance credibility: not computable, and that is the finding

MSTC has held quarterly earnings calls since roughly Nov-2022, hosted by a broker. Across the available record, only two forward, quantifiable commitments could be identified, and both are still open. There is no meaningful history of the company setting a number and being measured against it. A scorecard cannot be built from nothing, and the absence itself belongs in the governance column, not in the growth column. Section 08 scores the substitutes instead.

The three things that matter

Positive 1 · The toll booth itself is exceptional

289 employees produced about Rs 225 cr of operating profit in the twelve months to Jun-2026 on Rs 386 cr of fee revenue, a 58 per cent operating margin, with capital expenditure of Rs 22 cr a year. There is almost no capital in the operating business, so the return on the capital that is actually at work approaches infinity. Very few businesses in India look like this.

Positive 2 · The volume drivers are genuinely accelerating on official data

Mineral blocks auctioned went from about 90 a year across FY22 to FY25 to a record 212 in FY26. All-India coal production hit an all-time high of 1,047.6 million tonnes in FY25 with an FY26 target of 1,150.39 million tonnes. Domestic ferrous scrap generation rose 22 per cent to 32 million tonnes in FY26. On 03-Jun-2026 the Union Cabinet cleared a Rs 9,585 cr Delhi-NCR vehicle replacement scheme covering about 2.07 lakh vehicle owners.

Positive 3 · FY26's weakness was a price effect, not an activity effect

Management blamed the 36.65 per cent first-half FY26 fall in transacted value on softening scrap prices. Official trade data for the same year shows scrap volumes up 22 per cent and scrap-based steel output up 16 per cent. Physical activity grew; only the rupee value per tonne fell. That is a mean-reverting problem, and a price normalisation alone restores growth without needing any volume help. Q1 FY27 already shows the turn.

Risk 1 · Two of the biggest customers already built their own road

Indian Railways piloted MSTC's software in 2006-07, balked at a proposed 1.5 to 2 per cent commission, and built IREPS in house. It still runs it. The common bank asset auction platform MSTC originally built and the Finance Minister launched on 28-Dec-2019 is now owned and managed by PSB Alliance, with a rebranded portal launched on 03-Jan-2025. Coal India's current e-auction work order, dated 21-Mar-2025, covers only about 40 per cent of the work value. These are not hypotheticals. They are the base rate.

Risk 2 · FY26's revenue growth was mix, not franchise

Transacted value fell about 11 per cent in FY26, from roughly Rs 89,823 cr to Rs 79,737 cr. Fee revenue rose 18.9 per cent. The only way both are true is that the realised take rate jumped, from roughly 31 basis points to roughly 41. The most likely cause is the record mineral-block year, which carries richer economics than scrap. In Q1 FY27 the rate fell back to about 37.6 basis points. Growth bought with a policy-driven mix spike is not the same asset as growth bought with share.

Risk 3 · The balance sheet is not the fortress the screen says it is

Contingent liabilities of about Rs 1,517 cr as at 31-Mar-2025, of which roughly Rs 943 cr is disputed tax, sit against a net worth of roughly Rs 900 cr. Cash and investments of roughly Rs 1,150 cr to Rs 1,360 cr are almost exactly matched by non-debt liabilities of roughly Rs 1,073 cr to Rs 1,216 cr, which is the signature of a float business holding other people's deposits. Despite being effectively debt free, MSTC's credit ratings are CARE BBB+ and Acuite A+, which is the rating agencies telling you what they think of the litigation and the legacy receivables.

This is a bet on the Indian state continuing to outsource its own asset disposal to a 289-person toll booth, priced as if that mandate were permanent and about 10 per cent perpetual compounding were automatic, and it breaks if Coal India's March-2027 renewal goes the way Indian Railways and the bank auction platform already went.

Section 01

Industry and sector analysis

MSTC does not sit in one industry. It sits on the disposal side of several, and its revenue is the derivative of other people's production and procurement decisions.

The market MSTC actually serves

India's government digital transaction economy splits cleanly into two halves that are often confused. The buy side is procurement: ministries and public sector undertakings purchasing goods and services. The sell side is disposal: the same bodies liquidating scrap, surplus plant, mineral rights, coal, seized goods, non-performing loan collateral and surplus land. GeM dominates the first. MSTC operates mainly in the second.

That distinction is the single most important structural fact about MSTC, because the two halves are very different businesses. Procurement is enormous and free. Disposal is smaller and charged for.

The two barely overlap today: GeM is a buyer's marketplace with a deliberately low or nil fee, funded by the Ministry of Commerce, while MSTC is a seller's auction house that charges around 40 basis points. If policy ever decided to consolidate disposal onto GeM, the platform capable of absorbing it already exists, already has every ministry onboarded, and does not need to charge for the privilege. The second thing the table shows is more immediately useful: FY26 was the first year GeM's GMV has ever fallen, officially attributed to lower order value from central public sector enterprises. It is tempting to read a single shared government-activity slowdown into that. The stated causes do not support it, and this report does not make that claim.

On the price side, the picture inverts. MSTC's fee is ad valorem, so it is paid on rupees, not tonnes. In the first half of FY26 transacted value fell 36.65 per cent year on year to about Rs 30,167 cr, which management attributed to softening scrap prices. The physical market was growing at the same time. Volume and price are pulling in opposite directions on the same revenue line, and only one of them is disclosed each quarter. That tension is the single most useful thing to track.

Who captures the value

In this chain the seller (a ministry or public sector undertaking) owns the asset, the buyer (a mill, a trader, a recycler, a property investor) owns the demand, and the platform owns neither. MSTC's take is roughly 40 basis points of the clearing value. Indian Railways' revealed price for the same service, back in 2006-07, was that 150 to 200 basis points was too much to pay, and it built its own system instead. That single data point tells you more about the ceiling on this take rate than any rate card.

Regulatory architecture, and why the revenue is contestable

There is a widespread belief that public sector bodies are required to route disposals through MSTC. The evidence does not support it as a general rule. The Central Vigilance Commission has required, with effect from 01-Jan-2009, that even the selection of an e-auction or e-tendering service provider must itself go through competitive tender. MSTC does hold named nominated-provider status in specific pockets, notably for major mineral blocks and for minor mineral blocks in Assam, Chhattisgarh, Jharkhand, Rajasthan and Uttar Pradesh, and it has received discrete nominations such as the gold tariff rate quota auction in Nov-2025. But the largest single relationship in the book, Coal India, was won by tender in Mar-2025 and covers only about 40 per cent of the work value.

What that means in practice
  • MSTC's protection is a patchwork of specific nominations, not a blanket mandate.
  • Every large relationship is re-priced on a two to three year cycle.
  • A customer large enough to self-build has a live, demonstrated option to do so.
  • None of this is disclosed as a risk factor with any quantification in material reachable for this report.

Section 02

Business model and value chain

What MSTC does, segment by segment, explained without jargon. Two segments remain after Q1 FY27, and one of them is essentially the whole company.

Segment structure and what changed

MSTC has run three things historically. E-Commerce, the auction and procurement platform business, is the franchise. Trading, a legacy business in which MSTC took principal risk buying and selling physical metal and coal, has been in wind-down for years and management stated that the planned exit was completed in Q1 FY27, the quarter to Jun-2026. Ferro Scrap Nigam Limited, a wholly owned subsidiary recovering scrap from steel plants, was sold to Konoike Transport of Japan for Rs 320 cr against a reserve price of Rs 262 cr, with the share purchase agreement reported around 24-Oct-2024 and completion around Jan-2025.

Those two exits are why the reported revenue line is close to meaningless across time. Revenue from operations fell from Rs 721 cr in FY23 to Rs 316 cr in FY24, which is presentation, not collapse. Any five-year growth rate computed across that break is an artefact.

The most important paragraph in this report

In FY26, the value flowing across MSTC's platform fell 11 per cent while the fee revenue it earned rose 17 per cent. Arithmetically, the only way both can be true is that the realised rate rose sharply, from about 31 to about 41 basis points. The most plausible explanation is mix: FY26 was a record year for mineral block auctions, 212 against a run rate near 90, and a mineral block clears at a very different fee structure from a lot of scrap.

That matters because it changes what FY26's growth was. It was not share gain and it was not pricing power. It was a policy-driven change in what happened to be on the platform that year. In Q1 FY27 the realised rate fell back to about 37.6 basis points even as transacted value surged. An investor paying 21.9 times earnings for "18.9 per cent revenue growth" is paying for something that did not happen the way the headline says it did.

The float, which nobody talks about

Bidders must post an earnest money deposit before they can participate. For coal block auctions this is confirmed at Rs 200 per tonne, collected through a virtual account system and explicitly non-interest-bearing to the bidder. MSTC therefore sits on a large, continuously revolving pool of other people's money.

The balance sheet corroborates the scale even though the line item itself could not be read. Cash and investments sit at roughly Rs 1,150 cr to Rs 1,360 cr. Non-debt liabilities sit at roughly Rs 1,073 cr to Rs 1,216 cr. Those two nearly cancel. That is the arithmetic fingerprint of a float business, and it has two consequences that run through the whole of this report.

Consequences of the float
  • The "net cash" is largely not shareholders' money. Netting the full cash balance off the market capitalisation, as a screen would, overstates how cheap MSTC is. Section 14 handles this properly.
  • The treasury income is pro-cyclical, not defensive. Interest earned on the float is roughly a third of pre-tax profit. When auction activity slows, the fee falls and the float shrinks, so the interest falls too. The cash cushion contracts precisely when it would be needed.
  • Whether MSTC keeps the float interest at all is unconfirmed. Bidders demonstrably do not earn it. Whether MSTC retains it or passes it to sellers could not be established, and it is the single most valuable unanswered question about this company's earnings composition.

That gap is the central valuation question in Section 14.

Unknown unknowns, specific to MSTC

What a first read misses
  • The moat is administrative, and administration is revocable. MSTC's advantage is nomination and incumbency, not technology and not network effect in any protected sense. Two counterparties have already revoked.
  • Revenue is a derivative of other people's volumes. MSTC cannot create demand for coal auctions or mineral blocks. Its growth is a story about capturing a larger share of a pool that ministries and commodity cycles size for it.
  • The fee is ad valorem, so the business is quietly a commodity-price play. A scrap price cycle moves revenue directly with no volume change at all, and no sensitivity disclosure exists.
  • Platform obsolescence is unmeasured. Nothing reachable discloses the age of the underlying software, uptime, cybersecurity posture, or technology spend. For a business whose only asset is a portal, that is a conspicuous silence.
  • The legacy trading tail is not closed. The segment is exited, but a credit rating agency was still naming long-due receivables from the trading business as a live constraint in its most recent available commentary.
  • An exclusive partnership with a travel company was filed on 26-Mar-2026, and a travel business entry was flagged alongside the FY26 results. Nothing about MSTC's history explains this. It is the least grounded thing in the file and it has never been explained on a call that this report could reach.

Where MSTC ranks, and what the ranking hides

On profitability it is genuinely near the top. A 60.6 per cent net margin on 289 staff is second only to Indian Energy Exchange in this table, and Indian Energy Exchange is the single closest financial analogue in the Indian market: a small, staff-light, regulated transaction venue earning a fee on someone else's volume, with a policy-dependent mandate.

On valuation it looks cheap against the market-infrastructure cohort and fair against the public sector cohort. At 21.9 times it trades at roughly a third of CDSL and under half of BSE and MCX, and almost exactly in line with Indian Energy Exchange at 20.0 times. That last comparison is the honest one. Indian Energy Exchange is the market's standing verdict on what an Indian regulated fee platform with a contestable mandate and decelerating growth is worth, and that verdict is about 20 times. MSTC is not trading at a discount to its true peer. It is trading in line with it.

On scale it is the smallest thing here. Rs 386 cr of revenue against Indian Energy Exchange's Rs 632 cr and CDSL's Rs 1,239 cr. Market capitalisation of Rs 5,103 cr with a free float of about Rs 1,800 cr and roughly Rs 7.7 cr of daily turnover.

What competitors are pursuing

  • GeM has spent FY26 pushing into state government procurement, which grew 38.3 per cent, and micro and small enterprise supply, which reached 47.1 per cent of its GMV. Its central public sector enterprise business contracted. A platform whose easy growth is maturing is a platform with a motive to look at adjacent categories.
  • PSB Alliance has consolidated public sector bank asset auctions onto BAANKNET and, from 01-Apr-2025, the Insolvency and Bankruptcy Board mandated exclusive use of that platform for liquidation asset sales. That is a mandated, expanding franchise in a category MSTC helped create.
  • Indian Railways continues to run roughly Rs 2.65 lakh cr a year of procurement entirely inside IREPS.
No peer is in distress, so there is no share to pick up

The usual consolidation argument, that a weak competitor exits and the survivor gains, does not apply here. Every competitor named above is a government or bank-owned platform with no profit motive and no reason to exit. The competitive pressure on MSTC is one-directional.

Section 04

Competitive advantage: the 6 Cs

Assessed with evidence and a durability rating. Use the ratings control at the bottom right to strip the verdicts and read only the evidence.

1 · Cost
Weak

289 employees running a platform that clears Rs 80,000 cr a year is a spectacular cost position in absolute terms, and the 58 per cent operating margin proves it.

But a cost advantage is only a moat if a rival cannot match it. The cost of building a government e-auction portal is demonstrably low enough that Indian Railways built one rather than pay 150 to 200 basis points, and a bank consortium built another. There is no scale economy here that a determined counterparty cannot replicate.

2 · Capacity
Weak

Software capacity is not scarce. There is no plant to fill, no utilisation headroom to monetise, and no time-to-replicate barrier of the kind a manufacturing asset provides.

The one genuine capacity asset is administrative reach: empanelment and registration across many ministries and several states simultaneously. That takes years to assemble. It also takes one policy decision to bypass.

3 · Capital
Moderate

Effectively no meaningful borrowing, a large treasury book, and seven unbroken years of dividends including through the aftermath of a loss year. On the screen this is a fortress.

Two things cut against it. First, most of the treasury is float, not shareholders' money. Second, and more tellingly, the credit ratings are CARE BBB+ with A2 short term and Acuite A+ with A1+ short term. For a debt-free, cash-rich government company those are strikingly modest, and the stated constraints are receivables and litigation, not leverage. The rating agencies are not treating this balance sheet as a weapon.

4 · Channel
Moderate

The real asset in this business is the registered bidder pool. A seller does not choose an auction platform for its software; it chooses the venue where the buyers already are. Decades of registering scrap traders, mills, recyclers and property buyers is the one thing a new entrant cannot conjure.

It is also completely undisclosed. No bidder count, no repeat-participation rate, no realisation-versus-reserve statistic could be found. A moat you cannot measure is a moat you cannot underwrite. Rated Moderate on logic, not on evidence.

5 · Customer captivity
Weak

This is the weakest of the six and it is not close. The Central Vigilance Commission has required competitive tendering for e-auction service provider selection since 01-Jan-2009. The largest customer's current contract, dated 21-Mar-2025, explicitly covers about 40 per cent of the work value and runs only to roughly Mar-2027.

Two large counterparties have already left permanently. Customer concentration itself could not be quantified from any reachable disclosure, which is its own problem.

6 · Compliance moat
Moderate

The genuine article, in pockets. MSTC holds nominated service provider status for major mineral blocks and for minor mineral blocks in Assam, Chhattisgarh, Jharkhand, Rajasthan and Uttar Pradesh. It received a discrete nomination for the gold tariff rate quota auction in Nov-2025, a pollution control board approval for an electronic trading platform in Sep-2025, and a Karnataka excise department mandate in Oct-2025.

Each is real, each raises a barrier, and each was granted by an authority that can withdraw it. This is a licence book, not a patent.

The single most important moat source, and its half-life

It is the bidder pool, not the nomination. Nominations are how MSTC gets the listing; liquidity is why a seller keeps coming back, because a thin auction realises a worse price and a government seller is audited on realisation. That is the one advantage a new portal cannot buy.

Its half-life is roughly one contract cycle, two to three years, for any counterparty large enough to build its own venue and bring its own bidders. Indian Railways is the proof: it had enough scrap volume to create its own liquidity, so MSTC's pool was worth less to it than the commission cost. For the long tail of smaller state departments and public sector undertakings, the half-life is much longer, probably a decade or more, because none of them can assemble a bidder pool alone. The moat is durable against small customers and fragile against large ones, which is precisely the wrong way round for revenue concentration.

The return question, framed properly

The two named legacy exposures

A national audit report called an MSTC lending decision imprudent

A tribunal reportedly treated MSTC's own balance sheet note as an admission of liability

Long-running litigation with two banks, spanning the Bombay High Court and the debt recovery appellate tribunal across 2019 to 2023, includes a case summary in which the debt recovery tribunal directed MSTC to pay Rs 222.51 cr, on the basis of what the tribunal treated as an admission of liability contained in MSTC's own balance sheet notes. What the credit ratings are telling you

MSTC carries CARE BBB+ with A2 for short term, and Acuite A+ with A1+ for short term. That is the single most useful piece of third-party evidence available here, and it corroborates the direction of every flag above.

Concentration risks

MSTC's customer concentration could not be quantified because no major-customer disclosure was reachable. What is known is that Coal India runs two separate mandates with MSTC and is plainly the anchor relationship, and that the current e-auction work order covers about 40 per cent of Coal India's work value and expires around Mar-2027. Single-counterparty renewal risk concentrated into one date in March 2027 is the sharpest identifiable risk on the calendar. There is no plant risk, no single-geography manufacturing risk and no supplier risk, because there is no physical business.

Quantifying the scrap price sensitivity, approximately

MSTC does not publish a sensitivity and this report cannot compute an exact one. But the arithmetic of an ad valorem fee gives a clean approximation: because the take rate is charged on value, a given percentage move in the average realised price of what is auctioned flows through to revenue roughly one for one, with no offsetting cost. Operating costs are largely fixed at roughly Rs 145 cr a year, so the flow-through to profit is amplified.

On FY26 numbers, a 10 per cent fall in average auction realisation, with volumes unchanged, would cut revenue by roughly Rs 33 cr to Rs 37 cr and operating profit by almost the same amount, roughly 15 per cent of operating EBIT. That is the real beta in this business, and it is invisible in a margin table because the margin barely moves.

The one genuine tailwind that is being under-read

Official trade data for FY26 shows domestic scrap generation up 22 per cent to 32 million tonnes, consumption up 17 per cent to about 41 million tonnes, and scrap-based crude steel output up 16 per cent against 11 per cent for steel overall. Physical scrap activity grew strongly in exactly the year MSTC's transacted value fell. Management's own framing, that the problem was prices, concedes that activity was not the issue.

That matters for forecasting because it sets a lower bar than the headline suggests. MSTC does not need to win new mandates or grow volumes to recover its transacted value. It needs scrap prices to stop falling. Q1 FY27's transacted value of Rs 23,814 cr, which annualises above the whole of FY26, is consistent with that already happening.

Reverse discounted cash flow first, because the only honest starting question is what the current price already assumes. Then the forward case, then a sum of the parts that produces an uncomfortably precise answer.

Choosing the right multiple, and the trap underneath it

For an asset-light fee platform the natural primary multiple is enterprise value to operating profit, because it strips the treasury book and looks only at the franchise. For MSTC that choice immediately runs into the question everything else in this report has been building towards: what is the enterprise value?

The cash on MSTC's balance sheet is largely not shareholders' cash

Cash and investments are roughly Rs 1,148 cr to Rs 1,360 cr. Debt is Rs 145 cr. A screen nets those and reports about Rs 1,035 cr of net cash, a fifth of the market capitalisation, and concludes MSTC is cheap.

But non-debt liabilities are roughly Rs 1,073 cr to Rs 1,216 cr, and the balance sheet identity holds: net worth of about Rs 900 cr is almost exactly matched by non-cash assets of about Rs 950 cr less debt. The cash pile and the deposit liabilities very nearly cancel. That is the arithmetic signature of a float business holding bidders' earnest money, and MSTC's own auction terms confirm that bidders post non-interest-bearing deposits before they can participate.

The exact earnest money liability line could not be read, so neither extreme can be proved. This report therefore values MSTC on both enterprise values and lets the reader see the spread. It also states plainly which one it believes: given the near-exact offset, the float-adjusted case, in which shareholder surplus cash is close to zero, is the more probable of the two.

Pre-mortem and anti-thesis

It is 2029. The position has lost half its value. This section is written from there, sincerely.

The short seller's case, argued properly

  1. You own a toll on a road you do not own, and two of the largest users already built their own. Indian Railways piloted MSTC's software in 2006-07, refused a 150 to 200 basis point charge, and built IREPS instead. It still runs roughly Rs 2.65 lakh cr a year through it. The common bank asset auction platform MSTC built and the Finance Minister launched on 28-Dec-2019 is now owned by a bank consortium and rebranded. These are not warnings about what might happen. They are the completed base rate.
  2. FY26's growth was an accounting of mix, not a franchise result. Transacted value fell 11 per cent. Fee revenue rose 17 per cent. The realised take rate had to jump roughly 32 per cent for both to be true, and the most likely cause is a record 212-block mineral auction year against a run rate near 90. In Q1 FY27 the rate fell back to 37.6 basis points. The market is capitalising a policy cadence as if it were pricing power.
  3. The rate is contestable by law and already contested in fact. Competitive tendering for e-auction service providers has been mandatory since 01-Jan-2009. The Coal India work order of 21-Mar-2025 explicitly covers 40 per cent of the work value, so a competitor already holds the majority of MSTC's biggest relationship. That contract expires around Mar-2027.
  4. Roughly a third of pre-tax profit is interest on other people's money, and it falls exactly when you need it. Bidders post non-interest-bearing deposits. MSTC earns on the float. When auction activity slows, the fee falls and the float shrinks together. The treasury cushion that makes this look like a safe balance sheet is the most pro-cyclical line in the accounts.
  5. Contingent liabilities exceed net worth. About Rs 1,517 cr against roughly Rs 900 cr, of which Rs 943 cr is disputed tax. A tribunal reportedly ordered Rs 222.51 cr against MSTC on the basis of an admission it found in MSTC's own balance sheet note. A national audit report of 2021 called one lending decision imprudent, with Rs 220.84 cr unrecovered and no evidence of recovery since. The credit rating is BBB+ on a debt-free company, and the agencies say why.
  6. Operating cash flow was 23 per cent of reported profit in FY26 and nothing public explains it. There are three plausible benign explanations. None of them is disclosed. An investor is being asked to take the profit line on trust.
  7. Governance is outsourced to a ministry that could not fill board seats. Four separate exchange fines for board composition breaches, spanning quarters into FY26 even after a stated Apr to Jun 2025 fix that this report could not reconcile. The Director (Commercial) chair falls vacant on 01-Nov-2026. The board cannot appoint its own directors, which means nobody inside the company can fix this.
  8. Capital allocation is distribution plus one unexplained diversification. The entire Rs 320 cr of subsidiary sale proceeds went out as FY25 dividends. The only new direction announced since is a travel business, flagged with the FY26 results, preceded by an exclusive partnership with a travel company filed on 26-Mar-2026, with no stated capital, timeline or rationale, and never explained on a call this report could reach.
  9. The re-rating was bought with a headline, not a number. Up 57 per cent in the month after the 03-Jun-2026 Cabinet decision, on roughly thirteen times normal volume, into a float of about Rs 1,800 cr that is roughly 85 per cent retail. The economics reach MSTC through a joint venture that has never published a profit and loss.

Thesis killers

1 · The March 2027 renewal

Coal India re-tenders and MSTC's share falls below 40 per cent, or the rate drops materially. This is a single, dated, observable event and it is the most likely way the thesis dies.

2 · The take rate breaks 35 basis points

Two consecutive quarters with e-commerce revenue below 0.35 per cent of transacted value would confirm that competitive tendering has reset the economics permanently.

3 · A policy step extending GeM to the sell side

Any official move to consolidate disposal onto GeM ends the discussion. GeM is 6.3 times the size, already onboarded everywhere, and free.

4 · A contingent liability crystallises

An adverse final order in the bank litigation, or a material part of the Rs 943 cr tax book becoming payable, would consume a large fraction of net worth.

5 · Meaningful capital committed to travel

Any disclosed investment above roughly Rs 50 cr in a consumer travel business would reprice the capital-allocation assumption that underpins the entire dividend case.

6 · Operating cash conversion stays broken

A second consecutive year of operating cash flow below half of reported profit, with no explanation given, would move this from a disclosure gap to an earnings-quality finding.

The relevant historical comparison is not other public sector undertakings. It is Indian regulated fee platforms that re-rated on a policy theme. The ninety second case to buy

MSTC is a 289-person toll booth on the sell side of the Indian state. It earns about 40 basis points on Rs 80,000 cr to Rs 95,000 cr a year of scrap, coal, mineral blocks, seized goods and land that the government has no choice but to dispose of. It does this at a 58 per cent operating margin with Rs 22 cr a year of capital spending and no debt worth the name. There are very few businesses in India with this shape and almost none at this price.

At Rs 728 it trades at 21.9 times trailing earnings, 17.2 times operating EBITDA on the cash-netted view, and yields 2.2 per cent on a seven-year unbroken dividend record.

Every volume driver is accelerating on official data rather than on management commentary. Mineral blocks auctioned went from roughly 90 a year to a record 212 in FY26. Coal production is at an all-time high with a 10 per cent higher target. Domestic scrap generation rose 22 per cent. A Rs 9,585 cr Delhi-NCR replacement scheme covering 2.07 lakh vehicle owners was cleared in Jun-2026.

And the FY26 weakness was misread. Management blamed the transacted-value fall on scrap prices; official trade data shows the physical market grew strongly in the same year. Activity was never the problem. Only the rupee per tonne was. That is mean-reverting, and Q1 FY27 already shows the turn, with transacted value annualising above the whole of FY26, revenue up 21.7 per cent and profit up 37.5 per cent.

Horizon: two to three years, with the Mar-2027 Coal India renewal as the decisive waypoint.

The ninety second case to sell or avoid

You own a fee stream you do not control, on a mandate the law requires to be re-tendered.

Indian Railways left in 2006-07 over the commission and still runs its own system. The bank asset auction platform MSTC built now belongs to a bank consortium. Coal India's current contract covers 40 per cent of the work, and it expires around March 2027. That is not a risk list. It is a track record.

The growth you are paying for is not what it appears. Transacted value fell 11 per cent in FY26 while revenue rose 17 per cent, which only works because a record mineral block year lifted the mix. The realised rate has since fallen back to 37.6 basis points. Roughly 30 per cent of pre-tax profit is interest earned on bidders' deposits, which shrinks in exactly the conditions that shrink the fee.

Behind that, contingent liabilities of about Rs 1,517 cr sit against Rs 900 cr of net worth, operating cash flow was 23 per cent of profit with no public explanation, and the credit rating is BBB+ despite no debt.

And the last 57 per cent of the move came in four weeks on a Cabinet headline whose economics reach MSTC only through a 50:50 joint venture that has never published a profit and loss. On a sum of the parts, the price implicitly pays about Rs 500 cr for that joint venture and nothing at all for the litigation.

For an existing holder: the policy premium is the thing to sell, not the franchise. Revisit if the renewal lands intact and the take rate stabilises above 40 basis points.

This is a bet on the Indian state continuing to outsource its own asset disposal to a 289-person toll booth, priced as if that mandate were permanent and about 10 per cent perpetual compounding were automatic, and it breaks if Coal India's March-2027 renewal goes the way Indian Railways and the bank auction platform already went.
What this document does not do

It does not give a rating, a recommendation or a conviction level, and nothing in it should be read as one. Both cases above are argued as strongly as the evidence permits, deliberately. The evidence itself is also incomplete in specific, named ways set out in the executive summary and in the source list, and a reader who can open MSTC's annual report can close most of those gaps in an afternoon.

Execution: core versus new

Core auction and procurement · reliable

MSTC has been the venue of record for commercial coal block auctions across rounds numbering into the mid-teens, has renewed Coal India twice within eleven months, and executed a Rs 2,259.52 cr state land sale in a single quarter. It won four new categories between Sep-2025 and Mar-2026.

Where execution data exists, it is about the demand for what is being auctioned rather than about MSTC. In the 15th coal block round only 8 of 23 blocks drew bids, 21 bids from 16 companies. That is a market outcome, not a platform failure, but it caps MSTC's fee just the same.

New verticals · unproven or unmeasurable

The split is stark: MSTC executes reliably in the business it has always been in, and has published no completed delivery in any new vertical.

Sizing the platform above MSTC in the chain

GeM's FY26 gross merchandise value of Rs 5.03 lakh cr is 6.3 times MSTC's Rs 79,737 cr, and its cumulative Rs 18.4 lakh cr since 2016 is more than twenty years of MSTC volume at current run rates. Micro and small enterprises accounted for 47.1 per cent of GeM's FY26 volume across more than 51 lakh orders, and state government procurement on GeM grew 38.3 per cent.

The reframing this section normally produces, that a competition narrative is really a market-already-lost narrative, does not apply cleanly here, and saying so is more useful than forcing it. GeM is a buyer's marketplace; MSTC is a seller's auction house. The categories barely overlap today and no official statement of intent to extend GeM into disposal was found. What the comparison establishes is capability and precedent: the infrastructure to absorb MSTC's work exists, is already connected to every ministry, and charges nothing.

The more immediate competitive fact is narrower and better evidenced: Indian Railways runs roughly Rs 2.65 lakh cr a year entirely inside its own system. That is three times MSTC's whole platform volume, sitting permanently outside it, at one counterparty, because of a commission dispute two decades ago.

Where the official data and management's commentary disagree

Contradiction one, and it cuts in MSTC's favour

Management attributed the 36.65 per cent fall in first-half FY26 transacted value to softening scrap prices. Official trade data for the same year shows domestic scrap generation up 22 per cent to 32 million tonnes, consumption up 17 per cent to about 41 million tonnes, and scrap-based crude steel output up 16 per cent against 11 per cent for steel overall.

These are not inconsistent, since an ad valorem fee is geared to value and not to tonnage. But the framing conceals something useful. Physical activity on MSTC's platform was not falling. Only the rupees per tonne were. That sets a much lower bar for recovery than a volume problem would: MSTC does not need new mandates or more auctions to restore transacted value, it needs scrap prices to stop falling. Q1 FY27's Rs 23,814 cr, which annualises above the whole of FY26, suggests that has already begun. This is the most valuable forward-looking inference in the report and nobody appears to be modelling it.

Contradiction two, which is really a false pattern to avoid

GeM's gross merchandise value fell 7.4 per cent in FY26 and MSTC's fell 11.2 per cent in the same year. It is tempting to read one shared government slowdown into that. The officially stated causes do not support it: GeM's decline is attributed to lower order value from central public sector enterprises, a buyer-spend explanation, while MSTC's is attributed to scrap prices, a commodity explanation. Two different mechanisms, same direction, same year. This report records the coincidence and declines to make a causal claim.

Contradiction three, internal to MSTC

In FY26 MSTC's transacted value fell 11 per cent while its fee revenue rose 17 per cent and its reported profit fell 46 per cent. All three are true and all three point different ways. The profit fall is the FY25 disposal gain dropping out, and profit before exceptional items actually grew 22.84 per cent. The revenue-versus-volume gap is the take rate rising from about 31 to about 41 basis points on the record mineral block year.

Not one of those three headline numbers describes what happened to the business, and a reader who takes any of them at face value will reach the wrong conclusion. That is the clearest single illustration of why this company requires the filings that this report could not open.
